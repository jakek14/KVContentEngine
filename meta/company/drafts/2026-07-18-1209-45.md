## Post

Investors now require a 3:1 LTV:CAC ratio before writing a check for a D2C brand.

That number exists for a reason. A 3:1 ratio means every dollar you spend acquiring a customer comes back three times. It leaves enough margin to absorb platform cost inflation, higher CPMs, and one bad quarter without breaking the model.

Most D2C brands we talk to can't calculate this number accurately.

They have revenue. They have ad spend. But the math between those two points -- lifetime value per customer, average order frequency, return rates, 12-month retention curves -- is fuzzy at best.

The problem isn't the ratio. The problem is running a business where you can't precisely answer: are the customers I'm acquiring worth what I'm paying for them?

That question matters whether you're raising or not.

Build the LTV calculation first. Then look at what you're paying to acquire. The ratio will tell you whether your marketing is compounding or just keeping the lights on.

Save this before your next budget review.
