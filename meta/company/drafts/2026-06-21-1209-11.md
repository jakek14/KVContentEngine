## Post

77% of the largest SaaS companies now use consumption-based pricing.

The shift from "how many seats" to "how much value did this generate" is not coming. It's here.

Here's what changed: AI tools can now do the work that used to require headcount. When one AI agent does the work of five people, per-seat pricing penalizes the customer for becoming more efficient. That model doesn't survive.

The companies leading this shift aren't just repricing. They're rethinking what unit of value they actually deliver.

Datadog charges per host and per log volume. Snowflake charges per compute credit. Twilio charges per message. Each metric ties directly to something the customer can measure and attribute.

For software buyers: consumption pricing means more visibility into what you're actually getting. You pay for usage, not headcount.

For sellers: pricing that grows with customer success -- not with their org chart -- drives better NRR and better retention.

61% of SaaS companies now run a hybrid model: a base subscription with usage-based components on top. The pure per-seat model is becoming the exception.

If you're a SaaS company still running a pure per-seat structure, the question isn't whether this shift will reach you.

It's when.
