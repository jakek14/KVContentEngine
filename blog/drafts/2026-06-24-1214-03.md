## Title

When AI Is Your Customer: What Agentic Commerce Means for Founders

## Subtitle

Shopify just shipped the infrastructure for a world where AI agents do the shopping. Here's what that actually means -- and what founders need to understand before everyone else figures it out.

## Post

Shopify dropped 150 product updates last week.

Most of the coverage landed on the obvious things. Better checkout. Storefront search that handles typos. Shop Pay expanding to brands not on Shopify. Useful improvements, all of them.

The update that barely made the headlines is the one worth thinking about: Shopify Catalog and the Universal Commerce Protocol.

Those two things together are the infrastructure for a world where AI agents do the buying.

---

**What agentic commerce actually is**

For the past decade, the model has been simple: build a product, build a website, drive traffic, convert visitors. The human browses, the human decides, the human buys.

That model is starting to break.

Not completely -- not this year. But the direction is clear. AI assistants are becoming shopping agents. ChatGPT helps people find products. Microsoft Copilot recommends services. The AI assistant a B2B buyer uses daily is increasingly answering the question "what tool should I use for X" with a specific recommendation, not a list of search results.

When an AI agent is making or heavily influencing purchase decisions, the distribution question changes fundamentally. You're no longer just trying to reach the human. You're trying to be the product that the AI finds and recommends.

That's agentic commerce. And Shopify just built the infrastructure for it.

---

**The Universal Commerce Protocol**

Shopify co-built the Universal Commerce Protocol with Google. It's described as "the open standard for agentic commerce from discovery to checkout."

The practical effect: any AI that builds on UCP can discover, understand, and facilitate purchase of any UCP-enabled product. Shopify merchants are UCP-enabled by default. The merchant's specific checkout rules, discounts, and customizations travel with the product data.

Shopify Catalog does the distribution work. It structures your product information and syndicates it to ChatGPT, Copilot, the Shop app, and other platforms -- automatically, without additional apps or feeds.

One stat from the announcement: AI searches powered by Shopify Catalog convert at twice the rate of AI searches using scraped product data.

That gap is significant. And it reveals something important about how this is going to work.

---

**The product data problem**

Your catalog is probably not written for agents.

Human shoppers deal with friction. They can infer. They know that a "slim fit" shirt and a "tailored cut" shirt are probably similar. They can figure out that "for teams of 5-50" means they can sign up even if they have 7 people. They fill in the blanks.

AI agents are more literal. They're reading your product data and making decisions based on what's there. Incomplete specifications, vague descriptions, missing use cases -- all of that reduces the agent's confidence in recommending your product.

The 2x conversion rate for Catalog-sourced searches is almost certainly explained by the quality of the structured data. Better inputs produce better recommendations. An agent that has accurate, complete product information can make a confident recommendation. An agent working with scraped data full of gaps and inconsistencies can't.

This is the same logic that's been true about SEO for years -- the sites that wrote for how people actually search get more traffic than sites optimized for keywords that no one types. Except the gap compounds faster when the "reader" is making purchasing decisions, not just ranking decisions.

---

**The distribution question is changing**

Here's the version of this that matters most for founders building right now:

If an AI assistant is increasingly involved in your customer's decision to buy -- whether that's a consumer asking Alexa for a product recommendation or a B2B buyer asking ChatGPT which analytics tool to use -- then "distribution" has a new layer.

You still need to reach humans. That part hasn't changed. But you also need to be findable and recommendable by the AI systems those humans rely on.

What does that actually require?

First, structured product data. Not just a well-written product page, but data formatted in a way agents can consume cleanly. Schema markup, accurate specifications, complete descriptions of use cases, clear information about who the product is for and what problems it solves.

Second, authoritative presence in the places agents are trained on and pull from. Reviews on platforms that agents cite. Forum discussions where your product comes up organically. Content that answers the specific questions buyers ask before purchasing -- not content designed to rank for head terms.

Third, distribution infrastructure. Shopify's UCP is one example. The B2B equivalent will emerge in whatever form the enterprise AI assistants pull from -- probably a combination of G2, Gartner, analyst reports, and first-party integrations.

The brands that figure this out first have a window. It's probably not a 10-year window, but it's real for the next 18-24 months while the infrastructure is still being built and most competitors aren't paying attention.

---

**What this means if you're building in 2026**

If you're building a consumer product: your Shopify Catalog strategy matters now. The brands that structure their data for agent-readable syndication are already seeing the conversion lift. This isn't speculative -- the 2x number is from the Spring '26 Edition announcement.

If you're building B2B SaaS: the equivalent infrastructure is forming. G2 and Capterra have been optimization targets for years; in 2026 they're becoming agent-training inputs. Your product's presence in the places AI assistants learn from is a distribution channel, not a PR exercise.

If you're building anything: the question "would an AI agent confidently recommend this?" is becoming as important as "would a human landing on this website understand what we do?" Both are conversion questions. One is just newer.

---

**The buyer didn't change. The interface did.**

What buyers want hasn't shifted. They want solutions to real problems. They want confidence before they spend money. They want to find the right thing without doing too much research.

What changed is how they get to those answers. For a growing share of purchases -- especially in B2B, especially for software, especially for any product that requires comparison and research -- an AI assistant is now in the middle of that journey.

Building for that reality isn't about abandoning everything you know about marketing and distribution. It's about understanding that the conversation between your product and your customer now sometimes has an intermediary. And that intermediary is very good at reading data, very bad at dealing with gaps, and increasingly trusted by the people whose attention you're trying to earn.

The 150 updates Shopify shipped last week will help merchants sell better on Shopify. The two that matter most for the next five years are the ones that make your products findable by systems that are becoming the default answer engine for what to buy.

That's the one to pay attention to.
