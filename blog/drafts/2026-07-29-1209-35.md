## Title

Your LinkedIn Company Page Reaches 1.6% of Your Followers

## Subtitle

Company page organic reach dropped 66% in two years. Most founders don't know. Most are still posting like it's 2022.

## Post

Here's a number most B2B founders haven't seen: LinkedIn company pages now reach approximately 1.6% of their followers on an average post.

That's not a rough estimate -- it's the 2026 benchmark from Socialinsider's platform data, confirmed across multiple analysis sources. It represents a 60-66% collapse in organic reach since 2024.

If you have 5,000 followers on your company page, your average post reaches around 80 people.

I don't think most founders running LinkedIn company pages know this. And I don't think the ones who do fully appreciate what it means for how they're spending their time.

### What Actually Happened

LinkedIn has been systematically deprioritizing company page content in the feed since late 2023. The algorithm now allocates roughly 2% of users' feeds to company page posts. Personal profiles occupy around 65%.

Personal profiles now generate 561% more reach than company pages. Some research shows the same content -- identical text, same topic, same hashtags -- performing completely differently depending on whether it was posted from a personal account or a brand page.

This isn't a temporary fluctuation. LinkedIn made a deliberate structural decision to push brand accounts out of the organic feed. The rationale was user experience: people want to hear from people, not logos. The algorithm now reflects that.

### Why Founders Keep Missing This

Part of it is that the collapse happened gradually. If you've been posting consistently, you're comparing last week to the week before -- not last week to 2023. The drop is invisible when you're only watching the current trend line.

Part of it is that company page metrics look fine in the dashboard. Impressions, follower count, post views -- these numbers still go up and down in ways that feel like signal. But 1.6% reach means almost all of those impressions are from the same small fraction of followers seeing everything.

The other part is that "build a LinkedIn presence" often defaults to "build a company page" in the playbook most founders inherited. It made sense once. The channel worked differently in 2019.

### What Actually Drives Reach in 2026

The data is consistent across every source I've looked at: personal profiles are where LinkedIn still distributes content.

Employee-shared content gets 8x more engagement than the same content published from a company page. Founder posts that describe real decisions, mistakes, or observations perform better than polished brand copy -- not because people prefer raw content, but because LinkedIn's algorithm classifies personal-account posts as higher-value signals than company posts.

The companies with real LinkedIn audiences in 2026 almost all have one thing in common: a founder or executive posting consistently as themselves. Not on behalf of the brand. As a person who happens to be building something.

That's the distribution layer. The company page is downstream from it.

### What This Means for Founders Building SaaS

If you're a SaaS founder and your LinkedIn strategy is built around a company page, you have a structural problem.

You're publishing into a channel that reaches 1.6% of people who opted in to see your content. The 98.4% who followed you aren't seeing your posts. You're paying someone to write for an audience that mostly never reads it.

The move is to shift the gravity toward personal accounts. Not just the founder's -- any leader at the company who can speak credibly to the market and commit to showing up consistently.

The mechanics of this are less complicated than they sound. You're not repurposing company content onto personal accounts. You're creating content that only makes sense coming from a person -- real perspective, real decisions, real uncertainty. The company page can share, amplify, and link. But it's not the primary publisher.

### What to Keep the Company Page For

The company page isn't worthless. It's just not a distribution channel.

It's a credibility signal. When someone researches your company, they check the LinkedIn page. It should be clean, accurate, and regularly updated. But you shouldn't expect it to reach anyone.

It's also a paid channel. LinkedIn paid ads run from company pages. If you're running LinkedIn ads, the page setup matters. Organic reach is effectively zero -- but paid reach is not, and a well-maintained page improves ad performance and CTR.

And it's useful for events, job postings, and announcements that people search for directly. Those aren't feed-dependent -- they get found through search and profile pages.

But if you're posting content hoping to build an audience and generate pipeline through a company page, the math has stopped working.

### The Uncomfortable Truth

Personal brand is now the only reliable LinkedIn distribution most companies have.

That means the founders and leaders who are willing to post consistently, as themselves, about the real problems they're working on -- are the growth channel. Not a nice-to-have. The actual channel.

Some founders are uncomfortable with this. Building in public feels exposing. Not every week has a story worth telling. The line between genuine transparency and performative oversharing is harder to walk than it looks.

But the data is what it is. The company page speaks to 80 people. The founder speaks to 80,000.

That gap isn't going to close.
