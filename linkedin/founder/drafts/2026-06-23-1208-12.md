## Post

The new CAC Ratio hit $2.00 in 2024.

That means you're spending $2 in sales and marketing to get $1 of new annual recurring revenue.

That math doesn't work at scale.

There's a number buried in the same data that almost nobody talks about. The Blended CAC Ratio -- which includes expansion ARR in the denominator -- dropped from $1.61 to $1.40. A 13% improvement.

The difference between those two numbers is expansion. Customers who stayed and spent more.

When you include upsells, cross-sells, and seat expansion, SaaS unit economics still work. The problem is that most companies are measuring CAC the old way -- new logos only -- and wondering why the model feels broken.

It's not broken. Acquisition cost has just gotten too high to sustain on new logos alone. The business works when you build expansion in from the start.

Three things that actually move expansion revenue:

Activation before upsell. Customers who never fully use the product they bought won't spend more on it. Every expansion motion starts with making sure the current product is delivering real value.

Selling to the right first seat. The easiest account to expand is one that was sold correctly -- bought for one team, natural pull to the next. Bad-fit customers churn before they ever expand.

Success as a revenue function. Account managers who expand accounts aren't just building relationships. They're reading product usage signals and timing conversations accordingly.

New logos aren't the growth engine anymore. The companies that figured that out first are pulling ahead.
