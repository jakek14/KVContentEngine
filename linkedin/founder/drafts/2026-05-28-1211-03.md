## Post

Two years ago, $2M ARR was a strong Series A story.

That bar shifted.

The new standard expectation is $5M to $10M ARR before institutional investors take the meeting seriously. Not because they got greedier -- the comparison class changed.

For every company at $2M ARR today, there are AI-native companies that got there in months. Investors have more options. They're using them.

Here's what that actually means for founders in the middle:

The fundraising timeline stretched. Not because you're building poorly, but because the proof points required now take longer to accumulate at the new standard.

Burn rate becomes real. If you planned to raise at $2M and the bar is now $5M, you have 18 or more months of runway to figure out. That changes your hiring plan, your spend, and your priorities in ways most people don't think through early enough.

NRR is now table stakes. Investors don't just want ARR -- they want to see existing customers expanding. A $5M ARR company at 120% NRR is a completely different conversation than one at $5M with 85%.

The founders navigating this well stopped treating the Series A as a milestone and started treating it as an option.

Build the business that doesn't need the round to survive. Then the round becomes something you do on your terms.

What are most founders underestimating about the current fundraising timeline?
