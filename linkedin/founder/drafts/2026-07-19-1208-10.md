## Post

Every founder I know obsesses over product-market fit.

Almost none think hard about distribution-market fit.

Nike spent years building a best-in-class D2C operation. First-party data. Direct consumer relationships. No wholesale intermediaries cutting into margins. It looked like the future of retail.

Their digital business just dropped 12% as they pulled back into wholesale.

Turns out customers didn't want a direct relationship with Nike's website. They wanted Nike products available wherever they already shop.

Distribution-market fit means your buyers don't just want what you built -- they want it through the channel you're using to reach them.

Getting this wrong is slower and quieter than missing product-market fit. You grow, then plateau, then spend two or three years trying to figure out why acquisition is so expensive. The product tests fine. The demos go well. The unit economics just never quite work.

I see this in B2B SaaS constantly. A great PLG motion built for a market that wants to talk to a salesperson first. Outbound for buyers who search. Content for a market that doesn't read.

The product can be perfect. The channel can still be wrong.

Test distribution assumptions as aggressively as you test product assumptions. They're equally fragile.
