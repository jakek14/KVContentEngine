## Post

43% of SaaS companies now use hybrid pricing.

That number is projected to hit 61% by the end of 2026.

I don't think most founders are paying close enough attention to what's driving this shift.

Enterprise buyers want predictability for their core tools. They have budget approvals, annual contracts, and finance teams who need to know what software costs. Usage-based pricing makes that conversation harder.

But for AI features? The same buyers are actively requesting consumption pricing. They want to pay for what they use while they figure out whether they actually need the capability. They're not ready to commit to a seat count for something they're still evaluating.

So the split that's winning right now is a predictable base fee for the core product, with consumption-based pricing layered on top for AI and premium features.

One model for your stable product. A different model for the features your customers are still exploring.

Here's the number that keeps coming back to me though.

Companies that run regular pricing experiments grow 25% faster than those that don't.

Only 24% of SaaS companies run regular pricing experiments.

That means three out of four SaaS companies are leaving meaningful revenue on the table -- not because their product isn't valuable enough, but because they set a price once and never went back to test it.

Pricing is not a launch decision.

It's the highest-leverage product and revenue experiment you can run on a recurring basis. What does a $20 price difference do to trial conversion? What happens to annual contract rates if you adjust the quarterly discount? What would NRR look like if you introduced a usage tier for power users instead of capping their plan?

These aren't questions you answer by thinking about them. You answer them by running the test.

I've watched founders spend months optimizing onboarding flows to improve conversion by a few percentage points. The same energy applied to a pricing audit would likely move more revenue. And it takes less time to run than a full onboarding overhaul.

If you set your pricing more than 12 months ago and haven't revisited it, you're probably leaving money somewhere. The good news: you don't need a pricing consultant to find it. You need a hypothesis, a test, and 30 days.

Start there.
