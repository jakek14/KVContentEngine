## Post

SaaS valuations that once traded at 20-30x ARR now benchmark around 8-12x. That compression is real. Here's what it means if you're thinking about raising.

The companies raising at strong multiples in 2026 are the ones that can show efficient growth. Not just growth -- efficient growth. More revenue per dollar of CAC. NRR above 110%. Payback periods under 18 months.

The headline number -- $510B raised in H1 2026, a new record -- sounds exciting until you learn that 43% went to two companies. That's not a market signal. That's an anomaly with two company names on it.

For the rest of the market, the environment is tighter than it looks.

What this actually means for founders:

The bar for Series A has shifted. Early investors are now expecting 15-20% month-over-month growth at seed, where 8-10% used to move deals. If you're not there, more runway won't fix it.

The companies getting funded fast are the ones raising to pour gas on something that's already working -- not the ones raising to find out whether it works.

The question to ask before starting a fundraising process: do we have something that's clearly working, or are we hoping capital helps us figure that out?

If the answer is the second one, raise later. Or find a way to make it work with what you have. Efficient growth is something you can build toward. Burning runway trying to discover it is expensive.

The compression in valuations isn't punishment. It's the market correcting toward fundamentals -- which, if you're running a tight business, is actually good news.

The founders who understand that are making cleaner decisions right now.
