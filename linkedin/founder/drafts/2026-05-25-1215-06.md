## Post

Most founders treat "build in public" as a personal branding strategy.

I think that's backwards.

The most valuable thing about documenting your process publicly isn't the followers you build.

It's what writing forces you to figure out.

When you have to explain your product every week — what it does, why it matters, who it's for, why someone should care — you quickly discover what you actually know vs. what you've been assuming.

The posts that land tell you what resonates.

The ones you struggle to write tell you what you haven't figured out yet.

The resistance you feel when you can't quite articulate something clearly?

That's product signal.

---

Founders who've been doing this for years will tell you the same thing:

The audience shows up eventually.

But the clarity comes first — from the discipline of having to put the thinking into words where other people can see it.

That accountability changes how you think.

---

Build in public isn't primarily a marketing strategy.

It's a forcing function for product clarity.

The audience is the byproduct.
