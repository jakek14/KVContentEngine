## Post

The most expensive word in B2B marketing is "anyone."

As in: "This works for anyone with a website." "Any marketing team can use this." "Perfect for any business looking to grow."

The positioning might be technically true. But it's killing reach.

When you write for anyone, the algorithm sends you to no one. The ad doesn't know who to find. The content doesn't resonate because it wasn't written for a real person -- it was written for a category.

The founders building real audiences and real pipeline in 2026 all have one thing in common: they got uncomfortably specific.

Not "SaaS companies." The operations manager at a B2B SaaS company with 20-100 employees who manually runs status update meetings in Notion.

Not "e-commerce brands." The Shopify store owner running 3+ SKUs over $50 with a real email list but no system for post-purchase follow-up.

That level of specificity feels limiting. It's not.

The algorithm is better at finding a specific person than a broad category. The content lands because the reader feels like you wrote it for them. The referrals compound because every happy customer can immediately name someone exactly like themselves.

Specificity is positioning. And positioning compounds.

If you can't name the specific person who gets the most value from your product in one sentence, that's the first thing to fix -- before the next feature, the next campaign, the next channel.
