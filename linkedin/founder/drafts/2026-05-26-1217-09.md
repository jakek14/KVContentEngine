## Post

Here's what being a founder in 2026 actually looks like on a Tuesday:

Half your attention is on a product question that doesn't have a clean answer.

The other half is on a commercial problem that won't solve itself.

Both feel urgent. Neither resolves by end of day.

---

The social media version of this job is "I shipped a product in 48 hours using AI."

That part is real. The tools genuinely compress timelines in ways that weren't possible two years ago.

The part that doesn't get posted:

The product question from Tuesday is still open on Thursday.

The customer who was close didn't convert. The channel that was working needs to be rebuilt. The hire that felt right is taking longer than expected to get to full speed.

---

I'm not writing this to complain.

I'm writing it because the gap between the highlight version of founding and the actual experience of doing it is widening — not because founders are performing more, but because the tools making building faster haven't touched the harder parts of the work.

Knowing your customer deeply. Getting the positioning right. Staying clear about what matters when everything feels urgent.

Those parts are still manual. Still slow. Still require judgment that doesn't compress.

---

That's not a problem with AI. It's just what this work has always required.

In 2026, you can build faster than ever.

The question is still whether you're building the right thing — for the right people — with the right conviction behind it.

That one still takes a while to figure out.
