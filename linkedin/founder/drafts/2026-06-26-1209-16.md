## Post

The cost to build an MVP has never been lower.

The cost to get people to care has never been higher.

That's the actual state of bootstrapping in 2026.

AI tools collapsed development timelines from months to weeks. A two-person team can now build what cost a $500,000 budget five years ago. The access gap closed. Fast.

But here's the thing that did not change.

Distribution.

Getting the right people to know your product exists -- to try it, to tell others -- that problem has not moved. It was always the hardest part. Capital used to paper over it with enough paid acquisition to fake traction until something organic kicked in.

Bootstrapped companies cannot do that.

Which means the best bootstrapped companies I watch develop something most funded startups never build until the money runs out.

They find real distribution. Content that earns attention. Communities built around genuine problems. Partnerships that make sense to both sides. Word of mouth that comes from actually solving something.

That constraint -- no budget to hide behind -- forces a clarity of thinking that is hard to manufacture any other way.

The founders struggling most right now are not the ones who cannot build.

Building is the easy part in 2026. Anyone with a clear problem and the right tools can ship something real.

The founders hitting walls are the ones who built something good and still don't know who it's for, or how to reach them without paying for every single impression.

That part hasn't been disrupted.

AI changed what's possible to build. It didn't change what it takes to be found.
