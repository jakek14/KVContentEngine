## Post

A 5% improvement in customer retention can increase profits by 25 to 95%. Most founders are spending almost nothing on it.

Acquisition is loud. Retention is quiet.

You can see when a new deal closes. You feel it. There's a number that goes up. Churn is the opposite -- customers silently disappear, and the damage shows up weeks later when you're trying to figure out why growth stalled.

The math works against you at every stage.

SMB SaaS customers churn at 31 to 58% annually. That means some companies are replacing their entire customer base every one to two years just to stay flat. The top-quartile companies -- the ones with real unit economics -- churn below 10%.

The gap isn't luck. It's investment.

Three things that actually move retention:

1. Faster time to value. Most churn happens before the customer ever gets what they came for. The faster you get someone to their first real win with your product, the harder it is for them to leave.

2. Proactive outreach. Reactive support after someone cancels is already too late. Low-churn companies reach out before there's a problem -- asking what success looks like, not just whether they're happy.

3. Track the leading indicators. Churn is a lagging metric. Usage frequency, feature adoption, login patterns -- these predict cancellations 30 to 60 days before they happen. Build dashboards around the signals, not the outcome.

The median LTV:CAC ratio sits at 3.4:1. Top-quartile companies are at 5.6:1. That gap compounds over years and the difference isn't usually the acquisition channel.

If you want to move toward the top of that range, the fastest path isn't more ad spend. It's spending some time on the customers you already have.
