## Post

The most common pricing mistake I see from SaaS founders is not charging too much.

It's charging too little because they're scared.

There's a psychology to it. You're not sure the market will pay. You're afraid the objection is about price. So you price defensively -- low enough that price never becomes the reason someone says no.

But here's what actually happens when you're underpriced.

You attract customers who bought you because you were cheap. They're the highest maintenance, lowest expansion revenue, and they churn the moment a cheaper competitor appears.

The customers who pay more? They've already decided the product is worth it. They use it. They expand. They refer others.

Price is a confidence signal. Not just to your buyers -- to your team, your investors, yourself. When you price low, you're telegraphing that you don't fully believe in what you built.

The founders I've seen make big pricing jumps almost always report the same thing: conversion didn't drop. Sometimes it improved.

Because the right customer was never the one who needed a discount.
