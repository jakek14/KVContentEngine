## Post

35% of SaaS companies reported year-over-year revenue declines in 2026.

Average growth rate for the sector: 18%. And falling.

But the top quartile is accelerating. Not slowing. Actually growing faster while the rest of the market softens.

The separator isn't product quality or go-to-market spend. It's Net Revenue Retention.

Companies with NRR above 111% can afford to churn customers -- their existing base is expanding fast enough to offset losses. New business slowdowns don't sting the same way when your installed base is growing.

Companies sitting at 101% NRR are running a leaky bucket. New ARR in, almost as much out. Every quarter, marketing and sales work harder just to hold the line.

The instinct is to fix this by acquiring more. More leads. More campaigns. More outbound.

The actual fix is upstream: what are customers getting in their first 90 days that makes expansion feel like the obvious next move instead of an upsell the sales team has to engineer?

Revenue expansion is a product and onboarding question before it's a sales motion.

The companies that figured that out first are the ones growing while everyone else wonders what happened.
