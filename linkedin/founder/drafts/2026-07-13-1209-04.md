## Post

Everyone says "distribution is the moat in 2026."

They're right. But there are two types of distribution that look identical on a spreadsheet and compound completely differently.

Rented distribution: Instagram reach, LinkedIn feed impressions, paid ad clicks. You show up when the algorithm allows it. You pay when you want to reach people. Someone else owns the relationship, the data, and the decision about who sees your content next month.

Owned distribution: email list, SMS subscribers, direct relationships -- people who chose to hear from you, on a channel you control, regardless of what any platform decides to do next quarter.

Both can drive traffic. Both can drive revenue. Both look like "growth" in the short run.

The difference shows up at reset moments.

When Meta adds a 5% surcharge on European ad spend. When Google removes attribution models you built your reporting around. When a competitor outspends you on the platform you've been relying on. When an algorithm update cuts your organic reach in half overnight.

Owned distribution doesn't reset. Rented distribution resets every time the platform decides to change the terms.

Most of the "distribution" advice I see is really about building rented distribution more efficiently. That's useful. But it's not the same as the harder, slower work of building an audience that would notice if you disappeared -- and still finds you regardless of what the platforms decide to do.

The founders who win long-term aren't the ones who cracked the algorithm. They're the ones who built something the algorithm can't take away.
