## Post

The average B2B SaaS company now spends $1,680 to acquire one customer.

Only 44% are hitting the 3:1 LTV:CAC ratio that investors consider healthy.

Most founders know their CAC number. Far fewer know why it's that number.

Here's what I've seen get misdiagnosed constantly: the CAC problem gets treated as an ads problem. So the move becomes "optimize the campaigns." Lower CPC. Better landing page conversion. Tighter targeting.

Sometimes that's it. Usually it's not.

Usually it's one of three deeper problems:

You're acquiring the wrong customers. A buyer who churns in month 4 didn't have a $1,680 acquisition cost. They had a $1,680 mistake. Their LTV was negative. The unit economics were broken from the moment they signed up.

Your conversion funnel has a leak that more traffic won't fix. The problem isn't awareness. Something between "interested" and "paying" is falling apart. Throwing more ad spend at a conversion problem doesn't solve it -- it amplifies it.

Your ICP is too broad. When you try to speak to everyone in your market, your message lands for no one. The fastest way I've seen founders drop CAC isn't ad optimization. It's narrowing who they're actually talking to and writing specifically for them.

The companies in the top quartile -- CAC payback under 6 months -- almost always got there by figuring out which customers they DIDN'T want before they figured out how to find the ones they did.

The math on "acquire cheap, worry about retention later" eventually breaks. The math on "pay more for the right customer, then build for retention" compounds in your favor.

Most SaaS growth problems are ICP problems in disguise.
