## Post

Investors in 2026 aren't impressed by ARR growth alone.

The bar moved. And most founders are still pitching the old checklist.

Here's what I'm seeing as the real criteria now:

**CAC payback period matters more than CAC itself.**

Median SaaS CAC payback jumped from 11 months in 2021 to 18 months in 2026. Investors aren't writing checks to businesses with worsening efficiency -- they want that number trending down. Under 12 months gets real attention. Under 18 is the floor for a conversation.

**Net Revenue Retention tells the whole story.**

A 10-point improvement in NRR translates to a 20-30% valuation uplift. Companies at the top NRR quartile trade at 24x revenue. Bottom quartile: 5x.

Most founders treat expansion as a post-sale problem. Investors treat it as proof the product works.

**Cohort retention -- not aggregate.**

Aggregate metrics hide the truth. Anyone can mask churn with new logo growth. Investors pull cohort data because it shows whether the business is getting better at keeping customers over time or just covering the trend with volume.

The pattern I see most often: founders with strong numbers but weak explanations.

"We have 110% NRR."

"Great. What's driving expansion? Where does churn cluster? What does month-6 retention look like compared to 18 months ago?"

The number gets you in the room. The mechanism gets you the check.

If you're building toward a raise in the next 12 months: stop prepping the deck first. Start building the story behind why your numbers look the way they do -- the mechanism, not just the metric.

That's the conversation investors actually want to have.
