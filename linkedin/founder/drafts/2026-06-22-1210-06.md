## Post

The metric investors check first at Series A isn't ARR anymore.

It's NRR. And the benchmark is 110%+.

That means existing customers need to expand -- not just stay. At 110% NRR, your current base grows 10% per year with zero new customers. Below 100%, every churned account is a hole your new revenue is trying to fill.

Median NRR for SaaS at $1-10M ARR sits at 98%.

Most Series A companies are below the benchmark when they raise. Which is exactly why investors check it. They're not looking at the number -- they're looking at whether you understand why the number is what it is.

Founders who show up knowing their NRR and the specific places they lose expansion create a different fundraising conversation than ones who lead with ARR growth and hope the retention question doesn't come up.

Here's the part most founders underweight: NRR isn't something you optimize before a round. It's the consequence of product decisions made years earlier. Whether customers find more reasons to pay you more over time -- or fewer -- shows up in that single number.

If you're 12-18 months from a raise and haven't stress-tested your NRR, that's the work worth doing before anything else.

The investors writing Series A checks in 2026 are looking at retention cohorts before they look at growth charts.
