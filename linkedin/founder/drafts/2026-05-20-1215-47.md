## Post

Bootstrapped SaaS median growth in 2026: 15%.

90th percentile: 42.3%.

Same market. Same constraints. Three times the growth rate.

I've been thinking about what actually separates those two groups.

It's not founders who work harder. It's not even better product execution.

The 90th percentile bootstrapped founders went narrower, faster — and stayed there.

A generic CRM for SMBs vs. a CRM built specifically for solar installers.

A project management tool vs. a workflow system built only for general contractors.

The breadth that feels safe in year one is usually the same thing that caps your growth rate in year three.

Narrow market. Deep product fit. High switching costs. Customers who renew without needing hand-holding because the product was built for exactly their problem.

The founders compounding at 42% aren't building for everyone.

They picked one specific person with one extremely specific problem — and they solved it completely.

The hardest part of that decision isn't finding the niche.

It's committing to it when the broader market looks tempting.

What's the narrowest version of your market that you've been afraid to commit to?
