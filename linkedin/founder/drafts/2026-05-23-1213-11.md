## Post

Anthropic went from $9 billion ARR to $30 billion ARR in one quarter.

That's a 3x jump. In roughly four months.

Over 500 companies now spend more than $1 million annually with them. Eight of the Fortune 10 are customers.

This stopped being a startup story a while ago. It's an infrastructure story now.

Here's what I think it means for founders:

**AI spending is compounding, not slowing**

When eight of the ten largest companies in the world are paying seven-figure annual subscriptions to a single AI provider, this is core infrastructure — the same transition cloud made between 2012 and 2015. The companies that locked in early didn't regret it.

**The dollars have to come from somewhere**

Enterprise software budgets don't expand infinitely. Every AI dollar displaces something: legacy software, agency spend, headcount, other vendors. The SaaS companies that survive the next five years will be the ones that become more essential because of AI — not less.

**Switching costs compound fast in AI**

These aren't pilots. They're production deployments embedded in workflows. The longer an AI tool runs in an enterprise, the harder it is to remove. That's a flywheel for every company that gets in early.

For founders, the question is no longer "should we add AI?"

It's: "Where does AI make us so much better that customers genuinely can't function without us?"

The companies that answer that question in the next 18 months will own the next decade.

The ones still debating whether AI is hype will be reading about those companies instead of building them.
