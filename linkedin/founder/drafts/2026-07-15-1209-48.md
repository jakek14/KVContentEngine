## Post

Something crossed a threshold in July 2026.

Not a product launch. More like a shift in what the default assumption is.

92% of US developers are using AI coding tools daily. 41% of all code globally is now AI-generated. The word didn't exist 18 months ago and it's Collins Dictionary's Word of the Year.

Building software stopped being the hard part.

A solo founder can now prototype something in a week that would have taken a team months in 2023. The bottleneck that defined most of early startup life -- the technical build -- is essentially gone.

Which means the constraint moved.

What's hard now: getting anyone to care. Building an audience before you launch. Knowing who's paying attention to what you're making before you've committed fully to making it.

The companies that look like they have an unfair advantage right now didn't figure out how to build faster. They figured out that building faster only matters if you already know who you're building for.

The race to viable product is faster for everyone. Which means the moat went back to being distribution.

Vibe coding didn't change what makes a company valuable. It accelerated the timeline to the part that was always the real challenge.
