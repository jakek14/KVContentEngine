## Post

The platforms aren't going to give you back the data they've been taking away.

GDPR. CCPA. iOS 14. Cookie deprecation. Each one reduced what you could know about who was engaging with your ads and your content. Attribution got harder. Retargeting got restricted. Every major platform update made ownership of your own audience data less certain.

What didn't change: people still visit websites. Still show purchase intent. The signal is always there. The infrastructure to capture it -- and own it -- wasn't.

That's the conviction behind KnownVisitors. Not "let's scrape more data." Not "let's buy a list." Give companies first-party identity data from their own website, verified and compliance-scrubbed, in a format they actually own.

Two things I kept coming back to while building it:

The data you collect from your own site is the only marketing asset that can't be taken from you. Platform APIs change. Attribution models break. Cookie policies tighten. But the visitor who showed up on your pricing page -- that's a signal you earned. No algorithm update removes it.

The direction of travel on privacy is one-way. GDPR is eight years old and €5.88 billion in fines later. New CCPA rules took effect January 2026 -- new requirements on cookies, pixels, and data brokers. Building compliant from day one isn't just ethics. It's the only defensible long-term position.

Most founders I talk to are still operating as if third-party data is coming back. It isn't.

The companies that figure out how to own their audience -- site visitors, not just email subscribers -- will have a durability advantage that compounds over the next five years.

Your site. Your visitors. Your data.

That's the shift worth building around.
