## Post

The SaaS founders getting pricing right in 2026 have one thing in common.

They stopped charging for seats.

Per-seat pricing worked when value tracked with headcount. Each new user was a unit of value delivered. That math made sense.

AI broke it.

Intercom's Fin charges $0.99 per resolution -- not per login, per outcome. Salesforce Agentforce bills per conversation. Klarna's AI customer service runs the same model. These aren't experiments. They're the new default.

The model converging on: subscription for the floor, consumption for the ceiling. ARR stays predictable. Expansion revenue tracks with actual usage.

Three questions worth running against your current pricing:

Does your revenue grow when customers get more value from your product? If not, you're not capturing it.

Could a customer double their usage without it showing up in their bill? That's the leak.

Can a CFO defend "we pay per seat" when AI scales output without adding headcount?

Value-based pricing was always the right answer. AI just made it a lot harder to ignore.
