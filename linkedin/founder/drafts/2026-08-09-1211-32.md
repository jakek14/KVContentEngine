## Post

40% of the churn you think is a product problem is actually a payment problem.

That stat stopped me cold when I first looked into it.

Involuntary churn -- failed payments, expired cards, declined transactions -- accounts for close to half of all subscription losses in B2B SaaS. Unlike product-driven churn, it's almost entirely recoverable.

The problem is most teams treat it like a billing ops issue. Card fails, one automated email goes out, another customer lost. I've talked to founders who spent months redesigning onboarding to reduce churn when the real issue was sitting in their payment recovery flow the whole time.

Three things I've learned actually move the number:

Start before the card expires. A campaign that goes out 30 days before renewal -- reminding customers to update payment info -- recovers a significant portion of failures before they ever happen. That's the easiest win.

Run a real dunning sequence. Not one email. A series across email, in-app, and SMS over 7-10 days. Retries spaced over time consistently outperform hammering the card repeatedly on day one.

Flag high-value accounts immediately. When a $2,000/month account fails payment, someone on the team needs to know within the hour -- not at month-end churn review. The bigger the account, the faster the response needs to be.

The math is pretty direct. If 40% of your churn is involuntary and your current recovery process is a single automated email, the payback on actually fixing this is high relative to how much effort it takes.

Curious -- what does your payment failure recovery actually look like? Most founders I've talked to are surprised when they dig into the numbers.
