## Post

Free tools are working again as a SaaS acquisition channel.

Not the "free tier" of your product. An entirely different free tool that solves a specific problem your ideal customer has -- and drives them back to your core offer.

Here's why the math works:

Median CAC for SaaS is $1,200 right now. If you can drive 200 signups a month from a free tool that costs you $0 in paid acquisition, that's $240,000 in avoided CAC per month. Even if 10% convert to paid, you've paid for a developer's salary from inbound alone.

But most founders who try this build the wrong tool.

They build something impressive. Something technically interesting. Something they'd use.

Not something people are actively searching for.

The ones that work have a few things in common:

They solve a problem people already search for. "Check my email deliverability" gets searched every day. Build an email deliverability checker. The people searching for that are your ICP.

They're instant-value. No account required. You enter something, you get something back. Every extra step kills conversion. The fastest path from search to "oh wait, this is useful" is the path that works.

They leave an obvious gap. The free tool solves one piece of the problem and makes the bigger problem visible. If your core product solves the bigger problem, the transition makes sense.

One example pattern that shows up consistently: companies in the data space build a "check this one thing" tool that shows you how much you're missing on the full analysis. The free tool creates the realization. The product captures it.

The practical version of this for any SaaS founder:

Go look at your top five organic keywords. Now look at the search terms you're NOT ranking for -- the ones where people are looking for a quick answer to a specific question in your space.

That's your free tool idea.

Build it in a weekend. Ship it without a brand. See if it gets searched. If it does, add your logo.

Paid acquisition works. But $1,200 per customer is a rough place to start.
