## Post

The top quartile of bootstrapped companies reaches $1M ARR only 4 months slower than their VC-backed peers.

While keeping 100% of their equity.

I don't think enough founders are running this calculation.

Raising a seed round buys speed. You reach the same milestone faster, but you've sold 15-25% of your company to do it. The people who funded you now have a seat in every financing decision that follows.

For some companies that's exactly right. If you're in a land-grab market where 4 months behind means losing to a well-capitalized competitor, the equity cost is worth it.

But a lot of founders raise not because the math works, but because it's what founders are supposed to do. Build, raise, grow. Questioning that feels like admitting you're not ambitious enough.

That's the wrong frame.

Capital is a multiplier -- it amplifies outcomes in both directions. The question isn't whether you can raise. It's whether raising accelerates a business that already works, or adds pressure before you've found what works.

More founders are running this honestly in 2026. And a lot of them are deciding to wait.
