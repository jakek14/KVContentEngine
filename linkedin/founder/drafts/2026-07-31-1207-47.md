## Post

43% of SaaS companies now use hybrid pricing.

That number will hit 61% by year-end.

Outcome-based components are in 40% of enterprise deals today, up from 15% in 2024.

Here's the hot take: seat-based pricing is becoming a growth ceiling -- and most founders don't see it yet.

The problem with pure seat-based isn't the price point. It's what happens at renewal.

Your CSM walks into the conversation with adoption dashboards and seat counts. Neither tells you whether the software actually moved a business outcome. You're defending a renewal with activity data, not impact data.

Companies running usage-based or outcome-based components have a completely different conversation: "You used it this much. You drove this result. Here's how expansion pricing works as you scale." The upsell is woven into the model.

Think about what pure seat-based does to your NRR ceiling. Every year, the only levers are logo retention and selling more seats. But if your customer doesn't grow headcount, you can't expand. If the product delivered 10x value, you still bill the same. The pricing model doesn't capture any of the upside you created.

Meanwhile, a usage-based customer whose business grew 40% last year naturally expanded revenue -- without a renewal negotiation.

The companies with best-in-class NRR in 2026 aren't just building better products or running better CS programs. They've built pricing architecture that grows with value delivery.

Seat-based pricing made sense when the value of software was access to it. Software value now is about output. Charging a flat rate per head regardless of outcomes is increasingly a structural disadvantage.

If I were launching a new B2B SaaS product today, I wouldn't start on pure per-seat.

The pricing decision is also a retention decision. Build the model to grow with the customer -- not just to survive the first sales call.
