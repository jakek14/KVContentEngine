## Post

Intercom's "Fin" AI agent is on track to hit $100M ARR in early 2026.

That's not surprising when you actually look at how it happened.

Intercom spent a decade building customer relationships, deep integrations, and brand trust before AI arrived. Fin drops into an existing distribution system. Hundreds of thousands of companies already embedded in the platform, already paying, already trusting it.

The AI is excellent. But the moat isn't the AI.

This is what most founders get backwards right now. They spend months obsessing over model accuracy, feature completeness, the product roadmap -- and assume that if the product is technically superior, distribution follows.

It doesn't. It never has.

The AI space is full of technically excellent products nobody knows exist. And full of products that aren't the most advanced but have years of brand equity and loyal customers who aren't moving.

If you're building something AI-native: figure out your distribution moat before you finish the technical one.

The technical gap closes fast. It already is.

## Context

Intercom's Fin is one of the clearest examples of how distribution advantages compound in the AI era. The advice isn't to skip building a great product -- it's to point out that the founders winning right now aren't just the best engineers. They're the ones who understood that solving distribution first makes the technical work far more valuable.
