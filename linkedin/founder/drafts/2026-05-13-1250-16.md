## Post

The Rule of 40 used to be a metric you learned about in a term sheet.

In 2026, it's a metric you need to know before you take the meeting.

---

Quick primer if you haven't tracked it closely: the Rule of 40 says your growth rate plus your EBITDA margin should sum to 40 or higher.

30% growth + 10% margins = 40. Healthy.

50% growth + -10% margins = 40. Also healthy.

20% growth + -20% margins = 0. Problem.

The math rewards balance between growth and profitability. For years, the market let you ignore the margin side. High growth covered everything. VCs would fund losses if the top line was moving fast enough.

That window is closed.

Investors are now treating Rule of 40 as the line between "fundable" and "come back when you have more proof." The composition matters too — growth with deeply negative margins scores differently than balanced growth with a clear path to breakeven. Direction matters as much as the number.

---

Here's what I see when founders don't track this closely:

Most have one of two problems.

Strong growth, bad margins: the fix almost never starts with cutting costs. It starts with understanding which customers expand and stay versus which churn quickly. The CAC you're spending on customers who leave in six months is pure margin destruction. Fix retention and net revenue retention before you try to cut line items.

Solid margins, no growth: almost always a GTM problem, not a product problem. What worked to land the first customers isn't scaling. The acquisition motion that built your first half-million of ARR isn't building the next million. The answer lives in the go-to-market, not the product roadmap.

Both are solvable. But you can't diagnose what you haven't measured.

---

The founders who know their Rule of 40 number cold — and can explain both components and the trend line — walk into a fundraise from a position of clarity.

The ones who don't discover the number mid-process. That is not the moment to start doing the analysis.

Know your Rule of 40.

Know what's driving each side of it.

Know which lever you're actually pulling.

Then you can have a real conversation with anyone who matters about the business.
