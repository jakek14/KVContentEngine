## Post

35% of SaaS companies are shrinking year-over-year in 2026.

The average growth rate just dropped to 18% -- down across the board.

Most founders watch new customer adds. The dashboard shows signups, demos, new logos. It feels like motion.

What a lot of them aren't watching closely enough: NRR.

4% monthly churn sounds manageable. Run the math. That's roughly 40% of your customer base gone every year. You're replacing almost half your revenue just to hold flat. Every single month, you're in a race against your own attrition -- and the new sales numbers are covering the score.

The companies that actually compound aren't just acquiring well. They expand and retain well.

Top-quartile SaaS at 110%+ NRR grows 2.3x faster than peers sitting at 95-100%. Not 20% faster. 2.3x. The gap isn't acquisition. It's what happens after the sale.

Expansion revenue now accounts for 40-50% of new ARR for the top performers. The customer you already won is the best source of new revenue you have -- they already trust you, they've seen results, the sales cycle is a fraction of the cost.

When growth rate headlines fade, NRR either supports them or exposes them.

So a genuine question for anyone running B2B SaaS: when's the last time retention was the main agenda in a team meeting -- not a footnote after the new sales numbers, but the actual conversation?
