## Post

Most SaaS founders know their MQL count to the decimal.

Ask them their CAC payback period and you'll get a shrug.

That disconnect is where companies quietly bleed.

Customer acquisition cost has risen 40 to 60 percent since 2023 across B2B SaaS. Every industry. Every stage. The average buying cycle is now 134 days -- up from 107 days in 2022. Both numbers move in the wrong direction at the same time.

And yet the metric most founders report in their board decks is still pipeline and MQL volume.

CAC payback is simple to calculate.

Take your total sales and marketing spend in a period. Divide by new customers acquired. That's your CAC. Then divide CAC by average monthly revenue per customer. The result: how many months before that customer becomes profitable.

Investors doing Series A diligence right now expect payback under 12 months. That bar didn't come from nowhere. It reflects the real cost of capital -- longer payback means more months of sales and marketing spend required before a customer generates net positive cash flow. In a rising-rate environment with less patient capital, that matters.

Most founders who miss the payback benchmark don't know they miss it. They're tracking impressions, clicks, MQLs, and pipeline. Those are all leading indicators that can look good while the lagging reality quietly deteriorates.

The vanity metric problem in SaaS isn't that founders track the wrong things.

It's that they track the easy things. The ones that go up. And skip the ones that require a harder conversation.

CAC payback is a harder conversation. Which is exactly why it's the one worth having.
