## Post

The share of solo-founded startups rose from 23.7% in 2019 to 36.3% by mid-2025.

The shift is structural.

For most of startup history, solo founding meant one person stretching thin across product, marketing, sales, customer support. The capacity math never worked unless something got dropped. Teams were table stakes at almost every stage.

AI changed the math.

A founder who used to need a designer can iterate on visuals in an afternoon. Someone who needed a developer for basic integrations can ship them without writing much code. Marketing that once required a team to produce consistently can happen in a fraction of the time.

The constraint on solo founding was never effort. It was throughput. Throughput just expanded.

Here's the part worth sitting with: top-quartile bootstrapped companies now reach $1M ARR only four months slower than their VC-backed peers -- while keeping 100% of their equity.

Four months.

For most of the last decade, the bootstrapped vs. funded tradeoff was "you move slower, and you stay smaller." That framing is outdated. The time penalty for going it alone shrunk. The equity penalty for taking capital didn't.

The question isn't "can you build this without a team?" anymore.

It's "at what point does taking money actually make sense -- when you can go from zero to $1M without it?"

A lot of founders are working through that question right now.
