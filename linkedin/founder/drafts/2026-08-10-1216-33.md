## Post

Founder-led sales works. Until it doesn't.

Most SaaS teams hit the same wall somewhere between $800K and $1.5M ARR.

Sales has been you -- the founder. You knew the product cold. You knew which pain to poke in discovery. Close rates were solid because you were the one closing.

Then you hire your first AE. Or bring in a VP of Sales.

And the numbers go sideways.

Most founders diagnose this as a talent problem. Bad hire. Slow ramp. Needs more coaching.

It's usually a positioning problem.

When you were selling, you didn't need crisp messaging because your intuition filled every gap in real time. You could pivot mid-call. You knew when the prospect was warming and when to push.

A new hire can't do that. They need a clear, written answer to "why us vs the alternatives" that holds up without you in the room.

If that answer doesn't exist -- in a real document, not just in your head -- the handoff fails.

Taylor Sicard made this point well this week: the mistake usually isn't the VP hire itself. It's hiring before the sales story is portable.

Four things worth writing down before you make that hire:

1. Who exactly is the ICP -- not "SMBs in SaaS" but a specific description with firmographics and a pain profile in the customer's own language.

2. What problem they have -- in the exact words they'd use to describe it, not your words.

3. What alternatives they were considering and why each one falls short for this specific buyer.

4. Why now -- what made this the moment they decided to look.

Those four answers are your sales story. Pull them from your own close calls. Get your first 10 customers on a 30-minute call and ask them directly. Record it.

Then hand that document to every new salesperson before their first call.

The founders who do this work scale through the handoff. The ones who don't spend 18 months wondering why their sales hire can't close like they did.

The answer is usually that they were never given the material to do it.
