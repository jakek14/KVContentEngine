## Post

The metric that matters more than new customer count is the one most founders never look at.

It's not MRR.

It's Net Revenue Retention.

Here's why this reframed how I think about growth:

Public SaaS companies with NRR above 130% trade at 15–20x forward revenue.

Companies below 100% trade at 3–5x.

Same product. Same market. The only difference is whether the customer base grows or shrinks on its own.

The insight that actually changed my behavior:

Improving NRR by 10 percentage points adds more to your valuation than doubling new logo acquisition.

Most founders hear that and nod. Then go back to optimizing their outbound sequence.

The trap is that new logos are visible. You see the close, celebrate it, put it in the board update.

Retention is invisible until it's not.

By the time the churn curve is obvious in your dashboard, you've already lost a year of compounding.

The retention work that actually moves NRR isn't complicated: fast time-to-first-value in onboarding, usage signals that catch disengagement before cancellation, expansion paths embedded in the product.

But it requires showing up in the business month over month — not just closing deals and moving on.

The best growth lever most founders aren't pulling: keeping what they already have.
