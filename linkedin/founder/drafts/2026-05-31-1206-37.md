## Post

90% of bootstrapped startups that reach $1M ARR are still operating 10 years later.

That number surprised me the first time I read it.

The narrative around bootstrapping is that you're playing without a safety net. That funded companies have the runway advantage, the growth advantage, the talent advantage.

All of that is true.

But something happens when you build without capital. You have no room to hide.

Every feature has to earn its keep. Every customer who churns is money that doesn't pay the bills. You can't buy your way to growth -- you have to earn it.

That pressure is brutal. It's also what forces the thing most founders spend years avoiding: building a product people actually want, at a price they'll actually pay, in a way that actually keeps them.

VC money can mask bad fundamentals for years. Bootstrapping means the market gives you feedback in real time. There's no next round to paper over a bad quarter.

The founders who make it to $1M bootstrapped and are still running at year 10 didn't survive because they got lucky.

They survived because they had to.

What's the hardest thing about building without outside capital that nobody talks about?
