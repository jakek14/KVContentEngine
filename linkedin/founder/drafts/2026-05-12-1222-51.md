## Post

The average D2C brand loses $29 on the first sale.

Only 28% of first-time buyers ever come back for a second purchase.

Put those two numbers together and the business model gets uncomfortable to look at.

You're making a $29 bet per customer that they come back. And 72% of them don't.

---

The brands that survive this math aren't necessarily better at acquisition. They're better at knowing who's worth following up with — and they act while there's still a window.

There's a difference between a customer who came back to your site twice in the first 30 days after purchasing and one who went dark immediately. Same CRM status. Completely different likelihood of returning.

That behavioral layer — who's actively revisiting versus who's already moved on — is where the real retention signal lives. Before they decide. Before the window closes.

KnownVisitors surfaces who's back on your site between purchases, so you're not guessing who to prioritize in your win-back sequences.

---

The first sale is a cost.

The second sale is where the business starts.

Everything before that is buying time.
