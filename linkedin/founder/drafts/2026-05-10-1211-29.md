## Post

True loyalty dropped to 29% in 2025.

Not claimed loyalty — that's still 68%. People say they're loyal. Their behavior tells a different story.

77% of consumers are now pulling their loyalty faster than three years ago.

The industry is responding with more points. Better reward tiers. Gamification. Referral bonuses.

That's the wrong diagnosis. You can't buy behavior that has to be earned.

---

The data that matters: customers with genuine emotional connections to a brand show 306% higher lifetime value than transactional customers. Not a marginal improvement — a category-level difference.

Here's what the companies with high retention actually do differently:

**They know the specific person, not the demographic.** Not "25-34, urban, household income above $80k." The person who comes back every two weeks, what they buy, what brought them back, what would make them stop. Real knowledge, not proxy signals.

**They recognize before they reward.** The customer who returned for the fourth time this month doesn't want more points. They want to feel like the brand noticed. Recognition is free. It's also the rarest thing most brands offer.

**They catch the signal before the churn.** By the time a customer cancels or stops purchasing, the behavior change happened weeks earlier. Return visits dropping off. Time on site contracting. Engagement going cold. The brands with low churn aren't reacting to the revenue signal — they're reading the behavioral signal that precedes it.

---

The brands treating loyalty as a reward program are fighting a war of attrition they can't win. Someone will always offer a better discount.

The brands treating it as a relationship have a moat that discounts can't touch.

Retention is earned in the micro-moments before the customer decides to come back — not after they've already left.
