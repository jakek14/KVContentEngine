## Post

California just brought its first enforcement action against a data broker under both CCPA and the Delete Act.

At the same time.

That's new. The Delete Act went live August 1 -- data brokers are now required to process consumer deletion requests every 45 days via a state-run platform. And the CPPA didn't wait long to use it.

Why this matters: stacking CCPA and Delete Act penalties isn't the same as either one alone. Combined CalPrivacy and AG civil penalties have already exceeded $24.6M since 2022. Dual exposure under both laws simultaneously is a different magnitude of legal risk for any company running outbound from broker-purchased lists.

If you're sourcing contact data from data brokers to fuel outbound: the contacts you have on those lists may have already opted out via the DROP platform. You won't know which ones until your deliverability tanks or someone files a complaint.

The enforcement era isn't coming. It got here.

When I built KnownVisitors, compliance wasn't a feature we added as an afterthought. It was the foundation we built on. Every visitor we identify gets scrubbed against email verification, DNC lists, TCPA, GDPR, and CCPA before it reaches the user. Clean, verified, owned -- not purchased from a broker who just got told to delete it.

First-party data from people who voluntarily came to your site is the one data source that regulatory pressure can't touch.

Build accordingly.
