## Post

The EU AI Act reaches full enforcement in August.

That's three months away.

If you're building AI into your product and selling to European customers, this isn't a future compliance problem. It's a current one.

---

Here's what actually matters for founders:

High-risk AI systems — anything affecting health, safety, access to services, or personal rights — face penalty exposure up to €35 million or 7% of global turnover.

But there are two kinds of risk most founders aren't tracking.

The first is obvious: your AI feature gets classified as high-risk.

The second is sneakier: your B2B customer uses your tool inside a high-risk workflow — even if you never designed it for that use case.

That indirect exposure is the one that catches people off guard.

---

Most founders I talk to are building things that don't obviously touch high-risk categories — analytics, automation, identification, scheduling.

Probably fine.

But "probably fine" is not a compliance posture.

The founders who are ahead on this did three things:

First, they documented what their AI actually does. Not the marketing version — the technical version. What data goes in, what decision comes out, who is affected by that output.

Second, they classified every AI feature against the EU AI Act risk tiers before shipping to EU customers. Not after. Before.

Third, they made someone specifically responsible for compliance. Not "the team." One person with clear ownership.

---

The EU AI Act won't kill innovation.

What it does is raise the floor on what "legitimate AI company" means.

That floor actually benefits founders who were building responsibly before it was required.

The founders who documented before they had to will be glad they did.

The ones who didn't will be doing emergency compliance work in July.

Three months is enough time to get ahead of this. It's not enough time to ignore it.
