## Post

80% of your website visitors read only the headline and the first sentence.

That's it. The features section, the testimonials, the pricing table, the FAQ -- those only get read by the people who already made it past the first two lines.

Which means the headline is the page.

Most founders write headlines about what their product does. "The all-in-one platform for X." "Automated Y for growing teams." Feature descriptions dressed up as value propositions.

The headlines that actually convert describe what the reader gets. The outcome, not the mechanism.

Benefit-led headlines outperform feature-led headlines by 27% on average. But most B2B websites still lead with the feature because features feel like proof.

The fix isn't complicated. Rewrite the hero headline as the specific outcome your best customers were after when they signed up. Not "we track your X" but "know exactly which companies are ready to buy before your competitor calls them."

Not what the product is. What life looks like after it works.

Your conversion rate isn't a design problem or a traffic problem. Nine times out of ten, it's a two-sentence problem.
