## Post

Through most of 2025, you kind of had to call yourself an AI company to get a serious meeting.

"SaaS founder" read as behind the times. Investors wanted AI-first, AI-native, AI-everything. The label mattered almost as much as the product.

That's shifting.

The category distinction turned out to be mostly noise. What investors -- and customers -- actually wanted was always the same thing: software that solves a real problem, reliably, at a price that makes the unit economics work.

AI made that easier to build. It didn't change what the software needs to do.

The companies now commanding 25-30x ARR multiples aren't the ones that added "AI" to their pricing page. They're the ones where AI fundamentally changed the product -- faster outputs, new capabilities, measurably better results for the customer.

And the companies getting quietly written off aren't "traditional SaaS." They're the ones that never had real product-market fit and hoped a label change would cover for that.

SaaS was never the problem. Building something people don't actually need was.
