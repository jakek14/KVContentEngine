## Post

Microsoft 365 prices went up July 1. Most companies are looking at 12-13% per seat. Some closer to 20% when everything tallies.

That number is sitting in CFO inboxes right now.

And it's changing how buyers look at every other tool in the stack.

The buyer who 18 months ago would sign after a good demo is now running your product through a procurement checklist. They want measurable outcomes, a clear timeline, and an answer to "what does it cost us NOT to have this?"

For founders building B2B SaaS, this changes how you need to sell.

Demos aren't closing deals the way they used to. Your positioning does more of that work now. Your website, your comparison pages, your case studies -- these handle the job your AE used to do in the first meeting. If the value isn't obvious before the conversation starts, you're already behind.

The "easy yes" is essentially dead for anything above $200/month. That price point used to get nodded through. Now it gets justified.

What this means practically:

Your pricing page needs to answer "is this worth it?" before anyone talks to you.

Your case studies need to show before/after in numbers, not in vibes.

Your first email after signup should start proving value -- not ask for a call.

Budget pressure creates clarity. For founders building products that are genuinely excellent at something specific and measurable, this is actually a good environment. The noise around "nice to have" tools is getting cut. The category gets cleaner.

If your product is obviously worth the price, this is your moment.
