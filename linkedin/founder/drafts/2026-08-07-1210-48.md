## Post

Most bootstrapped SaaS founders don't know revenue-based financing exists.

Not as a footnote. As a real alternative to VC.

Here's how it works.

You borrow capital and repay it as a percentage of monthly revenue -- typically 2-8% -- until you've repaid a capped amount, usually 1.3 to 1.5x what you borrowed.

No equity leaves the table. No board seat. No quarterly check-ins with someone who wants you to triple ARR in 18 months.

The model is designed for SaaS specifically because recurring revenue is predictable. The lender can model the repayment timeline. In a slow month, you pay less. In a strong month, you pay more. The cash flow hit scales with how the business is actually doing.

The ownership math is worth sitting with.

After a Series A, the median SaaS founder owns roughly 55% of their company. Founders who used RBF instead typically keep closer to 85%.

That's not a knock on VC. Venture capital is the right tool for a specific set of bets -- high-growth markets, capital-intensive distribution, businesses that need to outrun the competition with cash or die. Those companies should raise.

But a lot of founders raising VC don't actually need venture-scale capital. They need $400k to hire two people, improve the product, and reach the next milestone. For that use case, revenue-based financing is usually the cleaner deal -- less dilution, less distraction, more control over the timeline.

The global RBF market is expected to surpass $9.8 billion. That capital is out there. It just doesn't announce itself the way VC rounds do.

If you're bootstrapped, profitable or near it, and feeling the ceiling that comes from not being able to hire fast enough -- worth knowing this option exists before you default to fundraise mode.

## Context
