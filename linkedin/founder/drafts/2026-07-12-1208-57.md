## Post

Three state privacy laws took effect July 1.

Connecticut expanded its data privacy act. Now covers any business processing data from 35,000 residents -- down from 100,000. They also added a requirement: if you use consumer data to train AI models, you have to disclose that.

Utah added a right to correction. Consumers can now formally request that businesses fix inaccurate records within 45 days.

Most companies saw these headlines and handed them to legal.

That's the wrong instinct.

Your marketing stack is where most of this actually lands. Email lists, CRM data, retargeting audiences, attribution tools -- all of it touches requirements that didn't exist 12 months ago. And Connecticut's cure period is gone. If you're out of compliance, there's no guaranteed window to fix it before enforcement.

There are now 20 state-level privacy laws in effect across the U.S. Each with slightly different thresholds, timelines, and definitions of sensitive data.

This isn't slowing down.

The founders treating privacy compliance as a one-time checkbox are going to keep getting surprised. The ones building clean, consented, auditable data practices now are setting something up that compounds.

First-party data isn't just the safer option. It's the one that holds up.
