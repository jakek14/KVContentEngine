## Post

20% of voluntary SaaS churn traces back to poor onboarding.

Not a bad product. Not pricing pressure. Not a competitor.

The first two weeks.

Most founders treat onboarding as a post-sales problem. Product builds it, CS manages it, engineering supports it. Marketing got the user through the door -- its job is done.

That's the wrong frame.

The customers who churn in month 2 or 3 almost never say onboarding failed them. They say they didn't get enough value, or it wasn't the right fit, or nothing at all -- they just stop paying.

But the usage data tells a consistent story. They never hit the activation moment. They logged in, didn't reach the outcome they expected in the first session, and quietly checked out. No cancellation drama. No feature request ticket. Just gone.

The fix isn't a better exit survey. It's redesigning the first two weeks around one question: what is the single result that makes a new user feel like this was the right call?

Everything else in onboarding is noise until you can answer that honestly.

I've watched founders spend months optimizing the acquisition funnel -- ad copy, landing pages, conversion rates -- and never audit what happens to users in the first 30 days. That's backwards.

Acquisition gets a user to sign up. The onboarding window is where the unit economics actually play out.

NRR is built or broken in those early weeks. The difference between a company at 90% NRR and 110% NRR usually isn't the product. It's what happens after the first login.

If you don't know your 30-day activation rate, that's the first number I'd find.
