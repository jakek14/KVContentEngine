## Post

Most founders think pricing is a product decision.

It's not. It's a market positioning decision.

What you charge determines who you're competing with.

$0 competes with inertia.

$49/month puts you in the pile with every other SMB tool.

$500/month triggers a different question -- "is this worth a real budget line?" Different buyer. Different objections. Different sales cycle.

$5,000/month puts you in a comparison set you didn't choose, against enterprise solutions with procurement processes.

The price isn't just what someone pays. It's a signal about what category you're in, who your customer is, and what the product is actually supposed to deliver.

The trap most early-stage founders fall into: underprice to lower friction. It works short-term. The problem is you attract customers who chose you because you were cheap, not because you were right. Those customers churn the moment something cheaper appears.

Price for the customer you want at year three.

Not the one who's easiest to sign this month.

Your pricing is probably the most important strategic decision you're not treating as one.
