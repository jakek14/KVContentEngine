## Post

Every retargeting campaign you ran before 2024 was built on borrowed infrastructure.

The bill came due.

Third-party cookies are gone across every major browser. That means retargeting as most teams built it — follow the user across the web after they leave your site — is no longer working the way it did.

Here's the part I think is underappreciated: the degradation isn't obvious in your dashboards.

Your campaigns are still running. The audiences are still populating. The ROAS numbers still look like something.

But the match rate collapsed. 10,000 visitors to your site now translates to roughly 3,000 matchable users on ad platforms. You're retargeting a fraction of who actually showed up — and your attribution model doesn't surface that gap.

---

This has a direct implication for how smart founders think about their data strategy going forward.

First-party data is the only retargeting asset that doesn't expire or get deprecated by a browser update.

The teams who saw this coming started building first-party data infrastructure years ago — email capture at every touchpoint, behavioral signals from owned channels, identity resolution for known visitors. Their retargeting audiences are still full because they didn't rely on infrastructure they didn't own.

The teams who didn't are now scrambling to rebuild from scratch in a much more privacy-constrained environment.

---

The practical framework I'd use:

**Audit your actual match rates.** Don't assume the platform is matching who you think. Pull the raw numbers: how many visitors entered your retargeting window vs. how many actually made it into targetable audiences?

**Build owned identity layers.** Email subscribers, logged-in users, known customers — these are retargetable regardless of browser policy. Every touchpoint that captures a first-party identity extends your retargeting reach without depending on third-party infrastructure.

**Treat site behavior as first-party signal.** What pages they visited, how long they spent, when they came back — this data lives on your server. The brands winning at retargeting in 2026 are the ones who built systems to act on behavioral signals from their own site, not from a tracking pixel that no longer fires reliably.

---

The cookie era made retargeting feel easy.

It also created the illusion that you owned the data.

You never did. You were renting it from the browser.

Building on owned data isn't a workaround for the new environment.

It's what the strategy should have been all along.
