## Post

Maor Shlomo built Base44 with roughly $10-20K of his own money.

He sold it to Wix for $80 million. Six months later.

I keep coming back to that story not because it proves some formula -- it doesn't. Stories like this can mislead as easily as they inspire.

But it does tell you something real about what's changed.

A year ago, building a product real people would actually pay for required significant engineering depth. Real infrastructure. Runway. Teams to move at any real pace. The gap between "sharp idea" and "something in the market" was measured in months of burn, at minimum.

That gap has compressed.

Not because the product work disappeared. It didn't. Understanding what someone needs badly enough to pay for is still human work -- still the hard part. No tool does that for you.

But the execution layer -- the part that used to require 10 engineers and 18 months of runway to even get to a real test -- is genuinely compressible now. The distance between an idea and something real people are using got shorter for founders who know how to work with the tools available.

What I see in founders who struggle in this environment isn't the absence of those tools. They're using them. The problem is usually that they're moving fast toward a problem nobody actually has.

Speed is widely available now. The judgment about what to sprint toward -- that's still the constraint that matters.

The founders winning right now invested in understanding the problem first. Slowly. Carefully. Then used the tools to close the distance fast.

Build the clarity first. The execution can catch up.
