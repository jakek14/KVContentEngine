## Post

The average enterprise SaaS company has 118% Net Revenue Retention.

The average SMB SaaS company has 97%.

That gap explains more about why enterprise software commands higher multiples than any revenue argument ever will.

When existing customers spend more year over year -- without you spending a dollar to re-acquire them -- the math shifts entirely. Churn becomes one variable in an equation that can still net positive even as some customers leave. You can lose logos and still grow revenue from the base you have.

But here's what founders building for SMB miss: 97% NRR isn't a structural disadvantage you have to accept. It's a signal that your expansion motion doesn't exist yet.

Most SMB products have one pricing tier. Maybe two. No upsell path. No usage-based component that scales as the customer grows. No add-ons solving the next problem the same buyer runs into six months after signing up.

So every year, the only lever is new logos. And new logos cost money. And when acquisition costs rise, the whole model compresses.

The companies that eventually escape that treadmill don't do it by cutting churn to zero -- though that helps. They do it by building a reason for customers to pay more over time.

More seats as the team grows. More data volume as the business scales. An advanced tier with features they'll want in six months but don't need today. A professional services layer that solves the implementation problem.

You don't need enterprise contracts to build expansion revenue. You need pricing architecture that has somewhere to go.

Retention is only half the retention problem.

The other half is expansion.

If your pricing model has one tier and no natural upgrade path, you're building a business that requires you to run faster every year just to hold position. The expansion motion takes longer to build than you expect. Start before you think you need it.
