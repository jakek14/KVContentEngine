## Post

38% of new startups are now bootstrapped.

Up from 22% in 2015. That's not a gradual drift -- that's a structural shift.

I've been watching this play out in real time. VC money has concentrated so hard around AI that for most founders, the calculus on raising has completely changed. OpenAI and Anthropic absorbed 43% of global startup capital in the first half of 2026. That's not "AI is hot." That's a funnel -- and most founders are outside it.

For founders who don't fit the AI mega-round mold, the alternative to raising isn't "raise less." It's "don't raise."

And the data backs it up. The top quartile of bootstrapped SaaS companies reach $1M ARR just four months slower than VC-backed peers -- while keeping 100% of their equity.

Four months. 100% ownership.

The bootstrapped path is harder in year one. No war chest. No runway buffer. You build with constraint, which forces a clarity that capital can't buy.

But there's a version of success the VC narrative never sells: a company that's fully yours. Profitable. Built on your terms.

That version is becoming more common. And I think it's the right default for founders who don't fit the narrow lane that's getting all the checks right now.
