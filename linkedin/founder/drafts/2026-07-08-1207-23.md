## Post

60% of the D2C brands that raised Series A between 2019 and 2021 are either gone or trying to raise at a down round.

Not 20%. Not 30%. 60%.

I keep coming back to this number because it tells you something important about what actually separates the companies still standing from the ones that aren't.

It wasn't the market. It wasn't the category. It was retention.

The brands that survived didn't just acquire customers -- they kept them. 40% repeat purchase rate in Year 1 vs 15%. That was the separator, over and over again. The ones with 40% repeat could recover their acquisition cost faster, generate compounding revenue from their existing base, and actually build something with staying power.

The ones at 15% were on a hamster wheel. They had to keep spending to stay flat.

Investors in 2026 know this now. The pitch that used to close deals -- "we're growing 30% month-over-month, help us scale" -- doesn't work anymore. What closes deals now: cohort retention data, contribution margin per order, CAC payback under 6 months.

The funding environment didn't get harder. The filter just got smarter.

If you're building right now -- e-commerce, SaaS, anything subscription-ish -- your first question shouldn't be "how do I grow?" It should be "how do I keep the people I already have?"

Because the investors who will fund your next round are already asking that question.
