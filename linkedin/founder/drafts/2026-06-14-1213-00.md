## Post

AI-native SaaS tools under $50/month are retaining only 23% of gross revenue.

Above $250/month: 70-85%.

That gap is almost entirely switching costs. Not features. Not support quality. Not NPS.

The "AI tourist" effect is real. Users sign up for one specific task, complete it, and leave. The product worked exactly as advertised. And it still churned.

This is the uncomfortable version of "build something people want." People can want something for exactly one use. Complete it. Move on.

The moat in 2026 isn't what your tool does.

It's what it costs the customer to stop using it -- not in money, but in workflow disruption, in decisions that now happen manually, in signal they no longer have.

The durability question I ask about any SaaS product now: if a customer cancelled tomorrow, what would they actually lose that they couldn't replace in a week?

If the answer is "not much," that's the real product problem.

What makes YOUR product genuinely hard to leave?
