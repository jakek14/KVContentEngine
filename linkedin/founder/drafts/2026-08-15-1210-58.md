## Post

The metric replacing ARR in every serious investor conversation right now is LTV:CAC.

For a long time, growth rate was the only number that mattered. You were doubling? Great, we'll fund you. Nobody pressed hard on how much you were spending to get each dollar of revenue, or whether customers were actually sticking around.

That era is over.

What sophisticated investors -- and the best operators -- are tracking now is the ratio between how much a customer is worth over their lifetime versus what it actually cost to acquire them.

The benchmark showing up in due diligence right now is 3:1. Three dollars of lifetime value for every dollar of acquisition cost.

But here's what that ratio really means in practice:

Your acquisition cost needs to be a third of what a customer's worth to you over time. Not a tenth, not a half. A third. And when you actually count CAC honestly -- ad spend plus sales compensation plus SDR time plus tool costs plus onboarding hours -- a lot of companies discover their real CAC is two to three times higher than what they've been reporting.

The founders struggling most right now aren't the ones growing slowly. They're the ones who scaled acquisition before they understood the unit economics. They built a machine that fills the front door while customers quietly leave from the back.

Before you can fix it, you have to know both sides of the ratio.

On the LTV side: what does a typical customer actually spend over their full relationship? Not the theoretical contract value -- the real number, averaged across cohorts you've retained and the ones you've lost early.

On the CAC side: every dollar. Every human touch. Everything.

If you can't answer that cleanly, you're making acquisition decisions without the information that actually matters.

And before you raise, someone's going to make you answer it.
