## Post

The clearest business decisions I've made are the ones where I couldn't afford to be wrong.

That's not wisdom. That's just what happens when you bootstrap.

When there's no runway to burn through, every decision gets pressure-tested fast. Hire the wrong person? You feel it next month. Spend on a channel that doesn't convert? You know by end of quarter. Ship a feature nobody asked for? You'll find out because nobody uses it.

The VC path turns those mistakes into learning cycles. Which is valuable. But it also turns them into acceptable costs of doing business.

Bootstrapping makes them expensive enough that you tend to make fewer of them.

I'm not arguing against raising — the right capital at the right moment genuinely accelerates the right company.

But I've stopped treating the bootstrapped path as a limitation.

The founders I respect most in this space are the ones who know exactly what customers need — not what the investor narrative needs.

Those two things often overlap.

But when they diverge, the bootstrapped founder has a natural forcing function to stay on the right side of that line.
