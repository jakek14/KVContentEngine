## Post

Per-seat pricing is dying.

Gartner says 70% of businesses will prefer usage-based pricing over per-seat by end of 2026. Outcome-based components are moving from 15% of enterprise SaaS to 40%.

This is the biggest pricing shift since the transition from perpetual licenses to subscriptions.

---

Here's what most founders miss: pricing isn't a finance decision. It's a product and marketing decision.

Per-seat was always a lazy proxy for value. Easy to pitch. Easy to invoice. Easy to anchor in a proposal. But it doesn't actually reflect what a customer gets from your product.

A team of five using your product to drive $2M in revenue shouldn't pay the same as a team of five using it for occasional reporting. The seats are identical. The value isn't.

---

Usage-based models fix the alignment problem. You charge as they extract value. You grow as they grow. Your revenue curve starts to look like your customers' success curve.

But they create a new problem: if customers can scale down as easily as they scale up, NRR becomes harder to predict. Churn is faster when there's less friction to cut.

The answer most founders land on is hybrid. A base subscription to ensure floor revenue. Usage-based components to capture the upside. Outcome-based tiers for enterprise — "you pay more when we deliver X."

---

The real question isn't which model to pick. It's: are you pricing what you sell, or what you're worth?

Most founders price to compete with the nearest reasonable alternative. That's a ceiling, not a floor.

The founders who price on value extracted have a fundamentally different conversation with customers — and a fundamentally different retention curve. Customers who feel they're paying fair for the value they get don't churn.

Pricing is the single highest-leverage decision in SaaS. Founders spend months on feature roadmaps and almost no time on pricing architecture.

That's backwards.
