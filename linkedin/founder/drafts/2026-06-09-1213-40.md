## Post

Slack, Notion, HubSpot. Calendly.

All of them tightened their free tiers in the last two years.

The instinct is to say they got greedy. The more honest read: they figured out what free was actually costing them.

A free tier that creates genuine value without creating any pressure to upgrade isn't a growth strategy. It's a donation. Users stay. They don't convert. They consume support and infrastructure without contributing revenue.

The model replacing it is called "strategic freemium" -- free tiers designed to create real value AND real friction at the same time. Enough value that you'd tell other people about it. Enough limits that the upgrade eventually becomes the obvious move.

It's harder to architect than just giving the core product away. But it compounds.

When I was thinking through how KnownVisitors approaches pricing, this was the tension I kept coming back to. The product has to be genuinely useful -- useful enough that someone would mention it without being paid to. But the architecture has to make paid the natural next step, not an afterthought.

The free tier isn't the product. It's the sales channel.

If you're building with a freemium model and your free-to-paid conversion is sitting below 5%, that's where the work is -- not in the acquisition funnel.
