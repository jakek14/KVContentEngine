## Post

AI compressed the time to build from months to weeks.

Getting people to care -- that part hasn't changed.

I've watched founders celebrate shipping faster and then hit the same wall everyone hits. Finding who the product is actually for. Building distribution from scratch. Convincing the first 100 people it's worth their attention.

Speed to ship isn't speed to fit.

The build phase shrunk. The distribution phase didn't.

Which means the bottleneck moved. It used to be "can we build it." Now it's "can we find the right people and make them believe we solve their problem."

That shift matters for how you allocate your time as a founder. The hours you used to spend in the build phase are back. Most people I see spend them building more -- more features, more product, more infrastructure. The ones who compound faster spend them on distribution: figuring out who their customer actually is, where they show up, what language closes them.

That's why I obsess over who's visiting our site -- not just how many. Building KnownVisitors started with this exact question: anonymous traffic is noise unless you know who's behind it.

The hard things in building a company were never about the code. They were always about the people -- finding them, understanding them, earning their trust.

AI gave us back the hours we used to spend building. What you do with those hours is the question that still matters.
