## Post

The metric that separates a 24x acquisition multiple from a 5x one isn't growth rate.

It's what happens to revenue after you close the deal.

McKinsey analyzed 100+ B2B SaaS companies and found that top-quartile NRR companies trade at a median 24x EV/Revenue. Bottom quartile: 5x.

Nearly a five-fold gap in enterprise value — driven by a single metric.

---

Net revenue retention measures whether existing customer revenue grows, stays flat, or shrinks over time.

100% = zero net churn.
110% = expansion outpacing churn.
120%+ is best-in-class.

Median across B2B SaaS right now: 101%. Top performers: 111%+.

---

Most early-stage founders track MRR obsessively. That's the right instinct early.

The better question after $500K ARR: are your best customers buying more over time?

Below 100% NRR, growth is a treadmill. You're replacing lost revenue just to stay even.

Above 110%, growth compounds — and your valuation story changes entirely.

---

One early signal I watch before NRR is visible in the data: which customers return to the site on their own.

Not because of a retargeting campaign. Not because of a triggered email. Just because they remembered and came back.

That behavior shows up in traffic patterns before it shows up in renewal rates. It's the earliest indication of what NRR will look like at renewal time.

Surfacing exactly who those returning visitors are — by name and company — is one of the things KnownVisitors is built to do.

NRR isn't just a retention metric. It's a signal about how much value your product actually delivers.

The companies at 115%+ aren't better at sales. They're better at making customers successful enough to want more.

That's the whole game.
