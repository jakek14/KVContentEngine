## Post

60% of Google searches now end without a click.

In Google's AI Mode, that number is 93%.

If you've spent the last three years building SEO infrastructure to drive inbound leads, that's not a scare stat. That's a structural shift worth planning around.

---

The playbook most founders were sold on: write content → rank → get clicks → build pipeline.

That playbook still works. But the volume it produces is contracting year over year.

AI Overviews now appear in 25% of all US searches. Informational queries — the type that used to fill the top of the funnel — trigger AI Overviews 39% of the time.

Your content might still be ranking. It might even be getting cited. But the reader doesn't need to click anymore.

---

Here's what I'm watching from founders who are adapting:

They're building to be cited, not just ranked. AI citation creates brand familiarity even without a click — someone who sees your name in an AI answer and later types it directly into Google is a warmer prospect than any cold click ever was.

They're investing in channels that can't be zero-clicked. Direct outreach, community, referral, owned email lists. Email is having a quiet renaissance among founders who watched their organic traffic plateau.

They're doubling down on branded search. When someone already knows you and types your name, they click. That traffic is more durable than any informational keyword position.

---

The founders I see struggling are the ones who went all-in on informational SEO and are watching traffic decline with rankings intact. The issue isn't the content. It's the infrastructure underneath it.

The ones adapting aren't abandoning content — they're using it to build authority rather than clicks, and investing in parallel channels that don't depend on Google's algorithm staying favorable.

---

Curious where others are on this: are you feeling the organic traffic shift in your business? Have you changed how you think about content's role in your growth model?
