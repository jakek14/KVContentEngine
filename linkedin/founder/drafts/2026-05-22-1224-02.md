## Post

AI made "how to build" easy.

The competitive edge now is knowing what to build.

Every founder I've talked to in the last year has a version of the same story: they shipped something in hours that would have taken months two years ago.

Claude Code, Cursor, whatever the workflow — the barrier between idea and deployed product is basically gone.

But here's the thing nobody's saying loudly enough:

The ceiling on your business isn't your speed of execution anymore.

It's the quality of your judgment about what to execute.

You can now ship the wrong thing faster than ever before.

Building KnownVisitors made this concrete for me.

The tools let us move fast. But the question "should we build this?" got harder, not easier — because the cost of building dropped so much that almost anything felt justifiable.

The founders winning with AI aren't the fastest builders.

They're the ones with real clarity about what customers actually need — and they use AI to execute against that signal precisely.

The hard work of talking to customers, understanding real pain, making sharp product bets?

That's more valuable now than it's ever been.

AI doesn't do that for you.

It just makes being wrong about it more forgiving.
