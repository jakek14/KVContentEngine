## Post

43% of Gen Z start their product searches on TikTok.

Not Google. Not Amazon. TikTok.

I've been thinking about what this actually means for anyone building a company right now.

The obvious read is: "we need a TikTok strategy." And maybe. But the deeper thing is that the discovery stack is fragmenting in ways most companies haven't caught up with yet.

Ten years ago you could roughly say: people find things on Google. They research, they compare, they buy. That journey was traceable, optimizable, predictable. You built content for SEO. You ran Google Ads. You connected the dots.

That map no longer describes what's happening for a generation that's entering real purchasing power.

Gen Z discovers on TikTok. Researches on Reddit and YouTube. Validates on G2 or Glassdoor or Discord. Compares on ChatGPT. Might never visit your website until they've already decided.

By the time they do show up, they've completed most of the buyer journey on channels you don't own and probably aren't tracking.

The brands winning with this cohort aren't just running TikTok ads. They're building content that belongs to the platform -- native, specific, searchable within the algorithm. Not repurposed landing pages dressed up as reels.

For B2B founders especially: your buyer's age is changing. The people signing off on software budgets at growing companies are getting younger. If your entire go-to-market assumes "buyer Googles problem, finds us, books a demo" -- that assumption has a shelf life.

Discovery is fragmenting. The teams figuring out how to be present in the research phase -- wherever that's happening -- are building brand assets that will compound. Everyone else is running plays for a buyer journey that's already changed.
