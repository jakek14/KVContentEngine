## Post

The fundraising bar moved. Here's what it actually looks like in 2026.

Founders going into Series A conversations right now are walking out surprised. The questions changed.

Investors aren't leading with growth rate anymore. The first questions are about the efficiency underneath it.

Here's the scorecard:

NRR above 110%. Ideally 120%+. Not just retention -- expansion. Existing customers spending more over time is the clearest signal the product solves something real. Under 100% NRR means replacing churned revenue with new logos every quarter just to move forward. That's a treadmill, not a business.

LTV:CAC of 3:1 minimum, trending toward 4:1. Investors watched too many 2021 vintages collapse when CAC doubled. "We'll figure out unit economics at scale" doesn't close rounds anymore.

CAC payback under 12 months. Under 9 months changes the conversation. If your payback is longer, you need a clear reason why -- not a plan to fix it later.

Gross margins above 70%. Below that gets scrutinized for whether a services layer is hiding a different kind of business.

Annual growth of 80-120%. Still the ticket to the room. But growth rate alone doesn't close the round. Efficiency metrics set the temperature now.

The underlying shift: investors got burned by growth-at-all-costs in 2021 and 2022. Companies that raised huge on growth rate alone mostly disappointed when the market slowed. Now they want unit economics that hold even when growth stalls.

If you're planning to raise in the next 12 months, this is the scorecard. Not every number needs to be perfect -- but you need a clear story for each one. "Working on it" isn't an answer that moves things forward.
