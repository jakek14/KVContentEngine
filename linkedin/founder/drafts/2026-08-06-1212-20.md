## Post

92% of developers use AI coding tools every day.

29% trust what those tools produce.

That gap is the real story of software development in 2026. Not the speed. The speed is real. The gap is the part nobody's talking about.

We've absorbed AI into our workflows faster than we've built any framework for knowing what to do with what comes out.

---

I think about this a lot.

The velocity is genuine. Prototypes come together in hours that used to take days. I'm not skeptical of the speed gains.

But there's a verification tax almost nobody mentions.

The honest version of "I shipped this with AI" often means: I reviewed it, I tested it, but I didn't fully understand every decision it made. That's fine for a marketing page. It's not fine for an auth flow.

The founders getting tripped up aren't the slow adopters.

They're the fast movers who treated AI-generated code as finished work and stopped there.

---

What actually works is knowing exactly where your trust budget is.

For low-stakes surfaces -- UI components, copy variations, utility scripts -- let the model run. Review it, don't audit it.

For anything touching user data, payments, or security -- a human needs to read every line. That's not optional.

The developers doing this well aren't AI skeptics or cheerleaders. They're operators who've figured out where the oversight actually matters.

The framing I keep coming back to: AI is a co-pilot, not the captain.

Ship fast. Know what you're shipping.
