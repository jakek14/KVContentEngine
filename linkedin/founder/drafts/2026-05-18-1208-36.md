## Post

Every founder has a take on PLG vs SLG.

Most of them are arguing about the wrong thing.

Product-led growth is a great acquisition motion.

You let the product do the selling. Free tier, trial, freemium — people find you, try you, convert. Low CAC, high volume, self-serve.

The problem: it's hard to move upmarket. Hard to land enterprise deals. Hard to expand into accounts that started small.

Sales-led growth solves those problems.

You hire a team, build a process, go outbound. You can close bigger deals, build relationships, navigate procurement.

The problem: high CAC. Long cycles. No product feedback loop. Sales teams hunting forever instead of farming what the product already built.

The founders winning in 2026 stopped debating and started building the hybrid.

Product-led sales.

Product drives initial adoption — the free tier, the trial, the low-friction first step.

Then you watch.

When usage signals show up — power user behavior, expansion within an account, a visit to the pricing page for the third time — that's when sales comes in.

Not to pitch. To accelerate.

Product-qualified leads convert at 5 to 6 times the rate of marketing-qualified leads. Because they're already inside the product. They already understand the value. The job is to expand it, not explain it.

Hybrid PLS companies run 2x more profitable than pure PLG or pure SLG peers.

Not because they split the difference.

Because they use each motion for what it's actually good at.

The PLG vs SLG debate made sense when those were the only two options.

In 2026, that debate is the surest sign a founder hasn't updated their playbook.
