## Post

Most founders hire a CMO to solve a marketing problem they haven't defined yet.

The result is predictable.

The CMO arrives and asks for the ICP, the brand positioning, the channel breakdown. The founder says "that's why we hired you." The CMO builds something from scratch. It doesn't quite match what the founder knows to be true about the business. They disagree on the direction. The CMO leaves 18 months later.

Nobody was wrong. They just skipped the prerequisite.

The prerequisite is doing the marketing yourself first.

Not forever. Not to become a great marketer. Just long enough to understand who your customer actually is, what makes them buy, which channels surface the best leads, why people churn, and what the positioning that resonates actually sounds like.

You can't write a useful job description for marketing leadership without that. You can't manage toward good outcomes without it. You can't tell the difference between a CMO who's building the right thing and one who's building the comfortable thing.

The founders who build great marketing teams eventually aren't the ones who found a great CMO first.

They're the ones who ran the ads themselves. Wrote the emails. Sat on the sales calls. Until they understood it well enough to know exactly what kind of help they needed.

Then hired for it.

That sequence matters more than most people think.
