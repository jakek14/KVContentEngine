## Post

Google Demand Gen is a different kind of campaign.

Here's what B2B marketers actually need to know before adding it to the mix.

Unlike Search, Demand Gen doesn't wait for someone to type a query. It puts your ads in front of audiences -- on YouTube, Gmail, and Google Discover -- based on interest signals, not active intent. You're not capturing demand. You're creating it.

That's a fundamentally different job than what most B2B teams have run before.

Three things worth knowing before you test it:

**It's a top-of-funnel tool, not a conversion tool.**

If your funnel depends on capturing high-intent search traffic, Demand Gen works best alongside Search, not as a replacement. Think of it as the channel that gets your brand in front of people before they start searching -- warming an audience that Search converts later. If you measure it on last-click conversions, it will always look like it doesn't work.

**Creative is the targeting.**

Google's algorithm optimizes delivery based on how audiences engage with your creative. Feed it weak creative and it finds the wrong people, regardless of how precise your audience setup is. This is the opposite of Search, where the keyword does the targeting work. On Demand Gen, the ad itself is the signal. Invest there first.

**The downstream behavior matters more than the Demand Gen metrics.**

Clicks and impressions from Demand Gen are secondary signals. The real measure is what happens later -- do audiences who saw your Demand Gen ads show up in Search? Do they convert at a higher rate? Do branded searches increase in the windows after a Demand Gen flight? Set up the view to track the full journey, not just the last touch.

Demand Gen isn't for every B2B budget or stage. But for companies building awareness in a category that isn't fully search-activated yet -- where buyers aren't searching for you because they don't know you exist -- it's a tool worth understanding.

The question isn't whether AI-powered campaigns belong in your mix. It's whether you understand them well enough to measure them honestly.
