## Post

For the first time in history, Meta is projected to pass Google in worldwide ad revenue this year.

$243.5 billion to Google's $239.5 billion.

Growing at 24.1% vs Google's 11.9%.

That's not a small gap. And it tells a real story about where buyer attention is going -- and where smart marketing budgets should follow.

**Why Meta is winning**

Three things converged.

Reels delivered results that outpaced almost every ad format prediction through 2025 and kept growing. Advantage+, Meta's AI campaign system, got consistently better at finding buyers without heavy manual audience input -- which means more campaigns hit consistent performance without needing a specialist to run them. And Meta's AI creative tools lowered the friction for brands who used to struggle producing social-ready assets at scale.

Better distribution. Better targeting. Less setup friction.

The result: more advertisers getting results, which attracts more advertisers.

**What it actually means for B2B teams**

The objection B2B marketers reach for: "Meta doesn't convert B2B."

True -- Meta rarely closes a B2B deal on first touch. But B2B buyers don't switch off when they close LinkedIn. They're on Instagram, scrolling Reels, and seeing brand content there too.

Brand exposure on Meta builds the recognition that makes your email campaign open rates higher. Makes your outbound sequence land better. Makes your Google search ad feel familiar rather than cold.

Meta is a multiplier. Most B2B teams don't treat it that way -- yet.

The companies building Meta into their brand mix in 2026 are going to have a frequency advantage that compounds over time. The ones waiting until it "proves ROI" on last-click will be catching up to competitors who started building brand recall two years earlier.

**The practical question**

If your buyers spend any meaningful time on Instagram or Facebook -- and they do -- the question isn't whether Meta is worth testing.

It's whether you can afford to let competitors own that attention while you wait for proof that was always going to arrive late.
