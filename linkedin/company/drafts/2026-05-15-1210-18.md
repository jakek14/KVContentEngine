## Post

Most B2B teams are sitting on first-party intent data they've never operationalized.

The signals are right there. Pricing page visits. Return visits. Product page dwell time. Repeat sessions from the same person.

But without the identity layer — who those visitors actually are — the signals can't connect to action.

---

Here's what the research confirms in 2026:

First-party data from your own website is the most defensible signal in B2B. It can't be bought by a competitor. It can't be deprecated by a platform change. It belongs entirely to you.

Yet the first move most teams make when they want better pipeline is to buy expensive third-party intent data scraped from everywhere else.

The sequence should be flipped.

Get your own site signals working first. Layer in external data if and when needed.

---

Contact-level ABM — reaching the specific person who visited, not just the company — delivers 74% more booked meetings than account-level approaches. But you can't do contact-level ABM without knowing who's actually visiting.

The gap between "someone from Company XYZ visited your site" and "here is the specific person who visited and what they looked at" is the gap between a signal you can't act on and a pipeline you can actually work.

That's the gap KnownVisitors was built to close: identity behind every visit, before anyone fills out a form.

What's your team's current approach to first-party data — are you operationalizing site visit signals, or still primarily relying on form fills?
