## Post

The worst marketing teams in 2026 have the cleanest dashboards.

Impressions up. CTR steady. Cost-per-click within range.

The business is flat.

---

The job of marketing has shifted. And most teams haven't updated the job description.

For a long time, the implicit mandate was: spend the budget, hit the activity metrics, show the funnel is moving.

In 2026, CMOs are being asked a different question:

Does your marketing actually cause growth? Not correlate with it. Cause it.

---

That's a harder question to answer than it sounds.

Because most marketing infrastructure was built to measure activity, not causation.

You can show impressions, clicks, MQLs, pipeline influenced. But if you can't draw a clean line between a specific marketing action and a closed deal — the answer to "does this cause growth" is "we think so."

That's no longer an acceptable answer for the companies making it.

---

The teams building marketing infrastructure for causal proof are doing three things differently:

They're tracking which content touchpoints appear in the journey of won deals — not just in the pipeline.

They're building attribution models that connect organic signals to revenue, not just paid channel to last click.

They're using behavioral data from their own site — who visited, what they looked at, when they came back — as the mechanism that connects marketing activity to sales outcome.

---

The CMOs getting more budget in 2026 aren't the ones with the best campaign stories.

They're the ones who can explain the mechanism.

That's the shift. Vanity is out. Causation is in.
