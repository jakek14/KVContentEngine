## Post

D2C investors in 2026 have a new checklist before writing checks.

3:1 LTV:CAC. 40%+ gross margins. Cohort retention proof.

Not a nice-to-have. A hard floor.

Here's what that actually means for marketing teams:

The 3:1 LTV:CAC requirement means you can't optimize acquisition in isolation anymore. CAC only makes sense when it's measured against what that customer is worth over time — by channel, by cohort, by segment. Most marketing teams track LTV in theory. The best ones build it into every acquisition decision.

The cohort retention requirement means investors don't just want good numbers — they want improving numbers. Month 6 retention better than it was a year ago. LTV trending up. The marketing strategy that wins long-term isn't the one with the lowest CAC. It's the one that brings in customers who stay.

Three things the brands getting funded right now do differently:

1. They know exactly which acquisition channels produce customers with the highest LTV, not just the lowest CPC.
2. They track retention by cohort, not just overall — so they can see whether what they're doing is getting better over time.
3. They build the customer relationship before the sale, not just after — which lowers churn without increasing spend.

Even if you're not raising, this is the right way to build.

The companies that win the next five years won't be the ones that spent the most on growth.

They'll be the ones that kept the customers they earned.
