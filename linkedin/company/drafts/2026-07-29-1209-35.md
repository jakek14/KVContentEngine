## Post

92% of B2B buyers already have a vendor in mind when they engage.

That number reframes what marketing is actually for.

Most B2B marketing operates on the assumption that the buyer arrives undecided -- awareness campaigns, gated whitepapers, demo requests. Capture the lead, nurture them down the funnel. The model assumes you're introducing yourself to someone who hasn't heard of you.

Most of them already have.

By the time someone clicks "book a demo," they've done the research. They know your name. They've probably read your case studies, seen you referenced in a community, or heard you mentioned by someone they trust. The evaluation started before they ever reached out.

The shortlist gets built before the process starts.

So the question isn't how to win the demo request. It's how to become the name that's already in their head when the evaluation begins.

That requires a different kind of marketing. Consistent thought leadership. Content that earns trust over months, not campaigns. Showing up in the communities where buyers talk -- and saying things worth remembering.

It doesn't show up clean in a 30-day attribution report. But it's where the 92% are won.

The 8% arriving truly undecided are worth competing for. The 92% were decided before you ever sent a campaign.
