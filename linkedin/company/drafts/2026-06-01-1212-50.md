## Post

61% of tech CMOs are already rewriting their content strategy for AI search engines.

The question is whether you're among them -- and whether what you're changing is actually the right thing.

When a B2B buyer asks an AI which tool to use for a specific problem, the answer isn't based on search rankings. It's based on who has the most authoritative signal on that topic. That signal comes from different places than SEO did.

Original research is the clearest lever. AI engines cite sources with information nobody else has. If your company tracks proprietary benchmarks, runs original surveys, or has first-hand data from your customer base -- publish it. Paraphrasing industry statistics isn't enough.

Third-party presence matters more than most teams expect. AI doesn't just read your website. It reads everything that references you -- review sites, comparison pages, podcast appearances, community threads. The more your brand appears across those contexts with consistent, specific positioning, the stronger the authority signal.

The companies winning in AI search don't have bigger content teams.

They have a clearer point of view.

The teams still thinking about content as "SEO fuel" are optimizing for a version of search that's shrinking. The teams thinking about content as authority signals are building for the version that's growing.

What we publish and how we position it is now the primary lever for visibility with buyers we'll never touch with a paid ad.
