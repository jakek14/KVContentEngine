## Post

CCPA entered its most demanding phase on January 1, 2026.

One-click opt-out recognition. Granular consent per purpose. Visible rejection mechanisms with equal prominence to acceptance.

Most brands responded by updating their cookie banner.

That's technically compliant. It's also the minimum possible response.

Here's what most legal teams don't tell their marketing counterparts:

The data infrastructure required for compliance is the same infrastructure that enables better personalization, better segmentation, and better attribution.

Same work. Completely different framing.

Brands treating compliance as a legal ceiling are spending on lawyers.

The ones treating it as a floor are building a competitive moat.

Third-party data is getting more expensive and less reliable. Every enforcement action -- California just fined a company $1.35 million for a non-functional opt-out form -- raises what "compliant" even means.

Behavioral signals from direct interactions. Customer match lists built from owned relationships. Website data tied to actual visitor identity.

All of that gets more valuable as third-party options shrink.

The compliance deadline was the forcing function most teams needed to build a real first-party data strategy.

Most used it to update their banner.

The ones who treated it as a starting line are six months ahead on data quality now.
