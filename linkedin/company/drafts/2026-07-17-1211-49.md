## Post

The median SaaS growth rate is now 18%. And 35% of companies are shrinking year-over-year.

But the 90th percentile is growing at 42.3%.

Not a typo.

The gap between top performers and the field is wider than it's been in years -- and it isn't explained by market timing or product category.

The companies at the top have a narrower ICP. Not "anyone who might benefit" but a specific company profile with a specific problem they can't ignore. Narrow ICP means higher conversion, lower CAC, and lower churn happening simultaneously.

They also grow accounts after the contract signs. NRR above 110% means the revenue base compounds. Below 100%, every new deal is partly replacing a customer who churned. The math works differently and it shows up fast.

And they're running paid acquisition on proprietary data -- their own first-party signals about who visits, who converts, who stays. With median B2B CAC now at $1,200, broadcasting to cold audiences at scale doesn't work the way it did three years ago.

The pattern at the 90th percentile: own the data, narrow the ICP, grow the accounts you already have.

Most of the 35% shrinking are doing the opposite.

What's your biggest lever right now -- acquisition, retention, or expansion?
