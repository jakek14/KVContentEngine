## Post

Most B2B companies track accounts.

They know Company XYZ visited. They know someone in the finance department came back twice. They have an account-level intent signal.

What they don't know: who it was.

---

In 2026, person-level identification is outperforming company-level identification on B2B pipeline.

The reason makes sense once you look at how B2B buying actually works.

A company doesn't make a purchasing decision. A person does.

Usually a specific person who did independent research, convinced themselves first, then built the internal case.

That person visited your site before any form was filled. They read your case studies, checked your pricing, compared your documentation against alternatives — all without raising their hand.

If you only know the company was interested, you can't reach the person who matters.

If you know the person, you can.

---

The difference in how you respond changes everything.

Company-level signal: "Someone at Meridian Capital visited our site — let's try to get in front of their buying committee."

Person-level signal: "The VP of Operations at Meridian Capital visited our pricing page three times in two weeks — let's reach out to her directly, with context about what she was looking at."

Same company. Completely different conversation.

One is a cold reach-out to an unknown. The other is a warm, relevant follow-up to someone who has already done the work of getting interested.

---

The reframe: tracking accounts is a proxy for the real job.

The real job is identifying the people behind the accounts.

In B2B, the sale is still person-to-person — even when the invoice goes to a company name.

The buyers who matter are individuals who need to trust you before they'll raise their hand.

Build your identification strategy around people, and the accounts take care of themselves.
