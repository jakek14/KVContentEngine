## Post

The average B2B website converts 1.4% of visitors to leads.

That means 98.6 out of every 100 visitors leave without identifying themselves.

Most B2B marketing teams look at that number and go straight to optimization mode -- better CTA copy, cleaner landing pages, faster load times, A/B tests on the form. That work matters. But it's improving the visible fraction while the other 98.6% -- which includes some of your highest-intent buyers -- walks out the door unrecognized.

The question that actually changes how you think about this:

Who is in the 98.6%?

Not all anonymous visitors are equal. The person who visited your pricing page three times this week and then read two case studies is not the same as someone who bounced off a blog post in 10 seconds. Both show up in your analytics as "user did not convert." Neither shows up in your CRM.

The teams that close this gap consistently do a few things differently.

They treat high-intent page visits as pipeline signals, not just analytics. Time on pricing page. Repeated visits within a short window. Resource downloads without a form fill. These are buying signals -- they just don't look like leads yet.

They build retargeting audiences from demonstrated intent rather than generic site visits. The audience of people who hit your pricing page is worth more per dollar than the audience of everyone who ever landed anywhere on your site.

And they address the anonymous problem directly. That's where visitor identification comes in. KnownVisitors identifies who's landing on your site -- name, email, company -- before they fill out a form. The high-intent visitor who researched you and left without converting doesn't have to stay invisible.

The 1.4% visitor-to-lead conversion rate isn't a ceiling.

It's a starting point.

The companies getting ahead of this aren't running more traffic. They're getting more from the traffic they already paid for.
