## Post

60% of all new software code written in 2026 is AI-generated.

That means the tools market is moving faster than ever — and the gap between a real product and a well-dressed prototype is harder to see than it used to be.

For B2B buyers evaluating new software this year, the standard checklist doesn't work the way it did.

A compelling demo is easier to build. A polished onboarding flow is easier to ship. The product can look mature before the thinking behind it is.

The signal that actually matters hasn't changed. It just got harder to find.

---

**Retention track record is the only honest signal.**

AI-native SaaS tools under $50/month see 23% gross revenue retention. Established products see 85%+.

The gap isn't about price. It's about whether the product solves a real, recurring problem — or whether it solved the interesting version of the problem that made a good demo.

Before buying: ask for retention data. Month-6 customer count vs. original cohort. NRR. Churn rate by segment.

If the vendor won't share it, that tells you something.

---

**Product depth shows up in the questions the team asks you.**

A team that's spent years solving a real problem asks different questions than a team that's been iterating on features for 12 months.

They understand edge cases. They've seen what breaks. They can articulate what the product doesn't do and why.

In discovery calls, pay attention to what the sales team asks about your workflow — and what they assume.

Shallow products have shallow discovery. Deep products reveal themselves in what they want to understand about your operation.

---

**Roadmap depth is the third signal.**

The best SaaS products have roadmaps built from customer pain, not market trends.

Ask about what got cut in the last two quarters and why.

Ask about the feature that customers keep requesting that they've decided not to build — and listen to the reasoning.

The answer reveals whether the company is building from real insight or from what looks good to investors.

---

2026 is a great time to be a B2B software buyer.

There are more good tools available than ever before.

But the evaluation work hasn't gotten easier — it's gotten more important.

The products worth building long-term relationships with are still the ones that were built that way.
