## Post

84% of CMOs now use ROI as their primary budgeting metric.

Sounds disciplined. It's actually gutting B2B brand-building.

Here's the problem: ROI-based budgeting only funds what a short attribution window can measure. Paid search. Retargeting. Conversion-tracked campaigns. Brand work -- consistent positioning, thought leadership, content that builds recognition over months -- doesn't show up clean in 30-day reporting. So it loses budget arguments every quarter.

But brand is what makes the performance channels work.

Companies with strong brand recognition pay less per click. They convert paid traffic at higher rates. They close deals faster because the name means something before the sales conversation even starts.

The 30-day attribution model that defunds brand is the same model that credits the last click before a closed deal -- and calls that the full story.

That click happened because someone recognized the name. The brand investment that built the recognition never got the credit.

Performance without brand is a leaking bucket. You can keep filling it. The hole doesn't go away.

The B2B companies compounding fastest are treating brand investment as infrastructure, not a nice-to-have. And they're measuring it in CAC and win rate -- not last-click conversions.
