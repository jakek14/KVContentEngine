## Post

B2B acquisition just got objectively more expensive.

$1,200. That's the median cost to acquire a single B2B SaaS customer in 2026.

18 months. That's how long the median company now takes to recover that cost in revenue.

Most marketing teams respond the way you'd expect: optimize bids, tighten targeting, cut low-performing channels, test new creative. All reasonable. None of it changes the fundamental math.

---

Here's what actually changes the fundamental math:

The average B2B website converts 2–3% of visitors. Which means roughly 97 out of every 100 people who showed up — already interested enough to find you — leave without a trace, without a follow-up, without a connection to your sales team.

When CAC is $1,200, those 97 aren't just missed conversions.

They're your most efficient acquisition opportunity.

They already found you. You didn't have to pay to reach them. They were signaling interest before they ever raised their hand.

---

The reframe:

The answer to a $1,200 CAC isn't better ads on the front end.

It's recovering value from the traffic you already earned.

That means identifying who those 97 visitors are — name, email, company, contact details — so follow-up can happen before the interest fades and they move on.

That's what we built KnownVisitors to do: turn anonymous site traffic into identified leads, so your cost-per-acquired-customer reflects the full value of the traffic you've already invested in.

The companies winning on growth efficiency in 2026 aren't out-spending the competition.

They're converting more of what they already earned.
