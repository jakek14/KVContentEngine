## Post

Google deleted four attribution models this month.

If you're running paid search, your numbers are about to look different -- and it's not because your campaigns changed.

First click, linear, time decay, and position-based attribution are gone as of mid-July. Anything still configured with those models migrated automatically to data-driven attribution. Only data-driven and last click remain.

That sounds like a technical adjustment. It isn't.

Attribution determines which campaigns get credit for conversions. Change the credit model, and the algorithm changes which campaigns it bids up, which audiences it prioritizes, and where it sends budget. The campaign results look different because the algorithm is running on a different optimization signal.

Three things to do now:

Document what attribution model you were using before July 15. If it was one of the four removed, you need to know that before you read any July performance data.

Don't make major budget cuts this month based on apparent underperformance. A campaign that looks flat in July may be reflecting attribution shift rather than actual performance decline. They look identical in Ads Manager.

Give the data-driven model 60 days to calibrate before drawing conclusions. Data-driven attribution is genuinely better for most campaigns -- it just needs time to learn your conversion patterns under the new setup.

The good news: data-driven is the right model for most B2B advertisers running toward longer buying cycles. The transition period is the noise. What's on the other side should be more accurate.

The risk is reading July as a signal when it's mostly a methodology change.
