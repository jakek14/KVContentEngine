## Post

The average B2B website converts 2.9% of visitors.

Most companies see that and immediately want to fix their conversion rate.

That's not wrong. But it's the smaller opportunity.

Here's the reframe: the other 97.1% didn't leave because they weren't interested. Many of them spent real time on your pricing page. They compared your features against a competitor. They read a case study, bookmarked your site, and planned to come back.

They were evaluating. They just weren't ready to raise their hand yet.

The conversion-rate mindset treats that 97.1% as lost traffic. But lost and unidentified are two different things.

The visitors who don't convert aren't invisible. They're just unnamed. And unnamed doesn't mean uninterested -- it means you haven't met them yet.

Most B2B companies have poured resources into optimizing for the 2.9%. Better CTAs. Faster load times. Stronger headlines. Pricing page redesigns. All useful. All fighting over a thin slice of a much larger audience that's already on your site.

Zero effort has gone into the 97.1%. Not because companies don't care -- because until recently, there was no way to see them.

That's the gap we built KnownVisitors to close.

Not every unidentified visitor becomes a customer. But the ones who spent four minutes on your pricing page and left without a form fill? They already showed you intent.

You just didn't have a name.
