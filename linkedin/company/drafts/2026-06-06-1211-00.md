## Post

Meta just surpassed Google in global digital ad revenue for the first time in history.

$243 billion versus $239 billion. Meta's growth rate (24.1%) is running at twice Google's (11.9%). This isn't a blip -- it's a structural shift that's been building for three years.

For B2B marketing teams, the implication isn't "shift budget to social." It's more specific than that.

Google built its model on intent. Search captures buyers at the exact moment they're looking for a solution -- high conversion, predictable, measurable. The limitation: search inventory is capped by how many people are actively searching for your category right now.

Meta built its model on behavioral reach at scale. You don't wait for the search. You get in front of buyers while they're consuming content, and the algorithm has gotten good enough to find them before they know they're in the market.

Advantage+ doesn't target the audience you define. It identifies buyers from behavioral signals and finds them. If your creative clearly communicates the problem it solves and who it's for, the algorithm handles the rest.

The old B2B split -- Google for conversion, Meta for awareness -- is getting harder to defend.

The teams pulling ahead aren't choosing between the two platforms. They're using Google to capture existing demand and Meta to build the demand Google will later capture. Sequence over either/or.

The ad platform landscape just shifted. The question is whether your budget allocation shifted with it.
