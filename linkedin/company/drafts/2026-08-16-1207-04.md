## Post

44 US states now have comprehensive privacy legislation.

Third-party cookies are gone in most major browsers. Consent requirements are stricter than they've ever been.

And most B2B analytics stacks are still fundamentally built on the assumption that none of this would happen.

The result: marketers are running dashboards that look complete but have real data holes in them.

Which traffic sources are driving your actual buyers?

You don't fully know.

Which campaigns are closing deals, not just generating clicks?

You're approximating.

Which visitors came back three times before converting?

Gone. That session history lives in a cookie that got blocked or cleared.

The problem isn't that privacy regulation is bad. It's that the gap between "what we think we know" and "what we actually know" about our site visitors has quietly grown for two years -- and most teams haven't recalibrated their confidence levels accordingly.

Understanding who's actually on your site -- not inferred from cookies, but actually identified -- is becoming the foundation of a reliable analytics picture.

The data infrastructure built for 2020 isn't the same one that works in 2026.
