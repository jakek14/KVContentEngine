## Post

Vertical SaaS funding is down 42% year over year.

$2.69 billion raised in the first half of 2025. $1.54 billion in the same period of 2026.

That's not a slow market. That's investors repricing an entire category.

The logic behind the pullback: LLMs are making the core promise of vertical SaaS -- deep workflow automation for one industry -- look less like a long-term moat. Why fund "Salesforce for dental clinics" when a general-purpose agent might do the same job in 18 months?

The companies still getting checks have something AI can't easily replicate on its own: deep integration with proprietary industry data, real workflow lock-in, and retention numbers that prove the product is genuinely irreplaceable.

Integration depth is back to being a moat.

From where we sit, the message for vertical SaaS founders is clear: double down on what makes you hard to replace. Not the UI. The data relationships and workflow dependencies that take years to build.

That's the durable edge in 2026.
