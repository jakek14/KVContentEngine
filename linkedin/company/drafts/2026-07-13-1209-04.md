## Post

Most B2B websites measure traffic. Not signal.

Sessions, bounce rate, pages per visit -- these metrics were designed for a world where you couldn't know who was visiting. They're proxies for interest. Useful once. But the gap between what they tell you and what you actually need to know in 2026 is significant.

Three signals actually matter for B2B:

Who showed up. Not just a company -- the specific person. Name, role, email tied to the session. A VP of Marketing who spent eight minutes on your pricing page is a different follow-up conversation than a student writing a research paper. The session looks identical in Google Analytics. The value is not.

What they looked at. Specific page combinations map to intent. Pricing page plus product comparison page in one visit is a warm lead. Single blog post with no next click is a reader. Your retargeting shouldn't treat them the same.

How deep the engagement went. Total time on site, whether they returned within a few days, how far into the funnel their page sequence goes. Return visitors in a short window are almost always in active evaluation -- not just browsing casually.

Web analytics built for traffic optimization can't surface any of these. The tools that answer them are a different category from your current stack.

The shift from traffic measurement to identity and intent measurement is where the next generation of B2B marketing efficiency comes from.

The sessions are already happening. The signal is already there. Most companies just aren't capturing it.
