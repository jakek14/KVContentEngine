## Post

Google AI Overviews now show up on 48% of all searches.

The organic CTR for position-one rankings on those queries dropped from 28-34% down to 8-12%.

If your B2B pipeline depends on content-driven organic traffic, this isn't a blip. It's structural.

The response most teams are defaulting to is to publish more -- if traffic is down, create more content and see what breaks through. That's probably the wrong answer.

Here's what the data actually shows.

Specificity beats volume. AI Overviews tend to cite authoritative, narrow sources rather than high-volume category content. A 2,000-word guide on one precise problem in your space outperforms a 500-word overview of a broad topic. The question to ask: is this content so specific and useful that an AI would cite it as a source? If not, it won't drive traffic the old way, and it won't drive traffic the new way either.

Branded queries are holding. When someone searches for your company by name, there's no AI Overview sitting above the organic result. The teams holding their traffic are the ones whose buyers search for them specifically -- not just the category. That's a brand-building argument, not an SEO argument.

Transactional pages are performing differently than informational ones. Pricing pages, comparison pages, and case studies aren't being absorbed by AI answers because the intent behind those searches requires clicking through. Informational content -- how-to guides, industry explainers, educational posts -- that's what AI is replacing.

The practical implication: where you invest in content matters more than how much content you produce.

Companies losing 50-80% of organic traffic right now built for informational volume. Companies holding are the ones with a brand people search for and product content specific enough that AI can't fully evaluate it.

Content strategy isn't dead. The version that was really just SEO arbitrage -- that part changed.
