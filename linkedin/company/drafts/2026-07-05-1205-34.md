## Post

55% of marketers say first-party data is significantly more important today than it was two years ago.

Most of them still don't have it.

Here's the situation most B2B marketing teams are actually in:

They know their ICP. They've built personas. They've defined the segments they want to reach.

But when it comes to actually targeting those people, they're renting someone else's data -- platform audiences built on cookies, third-party intent signals, LinkedIn's lookalikes.

Data they don't own. Data built on a tracking infrastructure that's collapsing.

20+ US states now have active privacy laws limiting third-party tracking. Over 140 countries have data privacy legislation on the books. The deprecation of third-party cookies didn't end in one clean moment -- it's been a slow erosion across platforms, browsers, and regulations for three years.

The companies whose marketing held up through that erosion share one thing: they built their own data.

Not rented data. Owned data.

What does that actually mean?

Your email list. Your retargeting pixels with server-side tracking. The CRM enriched with real behavioral data, not demographic guesses. The leads you can actually contact because they opted in.

And -- the one most teams ignore -- your website visitors.

97% of B2B website visitors leave without filling out a form. But they were there. They checked your pricing page. They read your comparison post. They were in the research phase before the buying phase.

That's first-party data. It's just sitting there uncollected.

The CPL for teams who've built this infrastructure is holding flat or improving. For everyone else, it climbed another 11% last year.

We built KnownVisitors to make that visitor data collectible. Name, email, company, role -- identified from the visit itself, without requiring a form.

The companies winning in 2026 aren't waiting for leads to raise their hand. They're working the data they already have.
