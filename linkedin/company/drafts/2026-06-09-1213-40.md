## Post

Company pages on LinkedIn saw organic reach drop 60 to 66% between 2024 and 2026.

Personal profiles now generate 561% more reach than company pages for the same content.

This isn't a temporary algorithm tweak. It's a structural shift in how LinkedIn distributes content -- and it has real consequences for B2B marketing strategy.

Here's what it means in practice.

A strategy built around the company page -- branded posts, product announcements, case studies, campaign content -- is fighting the algorithm for every impression. That content reaches a fraction of what it used to. The company page is increasingly a destination for people who already know you. It's a credibility signal, not a discovery channel.

Personal profiles distribute differently. LinkedIn still rewards individual expertise. A practitioner sharing a specific insight, a founder making a concrete argument, an operator describing something they actually learned -- these get distribution that brand content doesn't.

The B2B playbook that's working right now looks less like "publish from the company page" and more like "build individual voices within the company, then let the company page amplify them."

That means getting founders, operators, and practitioners posting with genuine expertise. Not polished brand content dressed up as personal. Real perspective on real problems.

The companies winning on LinkedIn organic aren't the ones with the best-looking company pages. They're the ones who recognized that their people ARE the distribution -- and the company page is what you point someone to after they've already decided you're worth a conversation.

If you're still measuring LinkedIn success primarily by company page performance, you're tracking the wrong metric.
