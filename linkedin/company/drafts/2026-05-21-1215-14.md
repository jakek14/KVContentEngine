## Post

$260 billion in recoverable revenue sits in abandoned carts in the US every year.

70% of shoppers fill a cart and leave.

And most e-commerce teams are only recovering a fraction of it — not because their email sequences are weak, but because they can't reach the people who abandoned.

---

Here's the actual problem:

Email cart abandonment campaigns are among the highest-converting sequences in all of e-commerce. 50% of users who click an abandonment email convert.

But email only goes to people you've identified.

The majority of cart abandoners are anonymous. No email address. No cookie match. No retargeting audience.

They browsed your products, added to cart, spent real time on your site — and then left. Completely invisible.

Your recovery tools are working fine. The limitation is the input: you're only sending recovery flows to the small slice of visitors your system knows about.

---

The brands cutting into that $260 billion aren't sending better emails.

They're increasing the percentage of cart abandoners they can actually reach.

That means identifying anonymous visitors before they leave — capturing the name, email, and contact details of people who haven't filled out a form but have clearly shown purchase intent.

That's what we built KnownVisitors to do: surface the identity of anonymous website visitors so your recovery sequences can run on a much larger pool.

The flow doesn't change. The match rate does.

---

The teams winning on cart recovery in 2026 aren't just optimizing the sequence.

They're widening the top of the recovery funnel.

And the gap between recovery rate and revenue recovered is getting larger for every brand that doesn't.
