## Post

The global average website conversion rate is 2.35%.

The entire CRO industry -- A/B testing, landing page optimization, checkout redesigns, heatmaps, form simplification -- exists to move that number.

But here's what almost nobody asks: what happens to the 97.65%?

They came to your site. Browsed something. Left.

And you have no idea who they were, what they looked at, or whether they'd ever buy if you could reach them again.

Brands have built entire teams and six-figure tool stacks around helping 2.35% of visitors convert slightly better.

The 97.65% walks out the door invisible.

CRO is real work. It matters. Moving a conversion rate by half a point at scale changes revenue.

The gap in the strategy isn't the 2.35%. It's the assumption that the other 97.65% is unreachable.

Some of those visitors are your highest-intent prospects -- they just left before subscribing, filling a form, or booking a demo. The behavior is a signal. The contact behind it just doesn't exist in your database yet.

The brands starting to figure this out aren't replacing their CRO investment. They're filling in the other side of the equation.
