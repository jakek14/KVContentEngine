## Post

Most B2B SaaS companies are losing 2-3x more leads than they should be. Not at the top of the funnel. At the conversion stage.

Here's what the data shows.

A product-qualified lead -- someone who has actually used your product, hit a meaningful action, experienced the core value -- converts at about 25%.

A marketing-qualified lead -- someone who downloaded a white paper, attended a webinar, filled out a form -- converts at about 9%.

Same lead. Different relationship to the product. Nearly 3x different outcome.

The gap is signal quality. An MQL raised their hand. A PQL showed you what they care about.

Most SaaS companies know this in theory. The ones closing the gap do three things differently.

First: they shorten the trial window. Counter-intuitive but consistent in the data -- 7-day trials convert at 40%, 60-day trials at 30%. More time doesn't mean more confidence. It means more delay and less urgency.

Second: they define a specific activation milestone. Not "used the product." Something precise. For a project management tool, it might be creating and assigning a task to a team member. For analytics software, running a first report. The milestone predicts whether the user will retain at month 12.

Third: they align sales outreach to product behavior. Not to time. The sales team reaches out when a user hits the activation milestone, not 3 days after signup. That's the handoff that actually converts.

None of this requires a massive sales team. It requires knowing which users are ready -- and acting on it.

The companies running hybrid PLG-plus-sales motions right now report 2x higher profitability than either pure self-serve or pure sales-led peers. Because they only spend sales resources on the leads worth spending them on.
