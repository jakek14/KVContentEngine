## Post

92% of B2B buyers already have a vendor in mind before they talk to sales.

That number is from Forrester. And most B2B companies are still organizing their entire growth strategy around the 8%.

Here's the logic most teams are running, whether they've said it out loud or not:

Sales reps persuade buyers. Marketing fills the pipeline. The conversion happens in the conversation.

That's wrong.

Buyers don't arrive at sales calls undecided, waiting to be convinced. They arrive with a shortlist already formed. Usually 2-3 options, one of which they quietly prefer. The sales call is where they look for a reason to confirm that preference -- or a reason to panic and reconsider.

If you're not on the shortlist before the first call, you're not winning the deal. You're being kept around to validate the price.

This happens in the dark.

Not on a discovery call. Not in a demo. It happens when a buyer reads your LinkedIn posts, stumbles on your founder's content, finds a case study that sounds like their exact problem, talks to a peer who mentioned you at a conference. Consideration gets built before anyone picks up the phone.

The companies winning the most B2B deals right now aren't the ones with the best sales teams.

They're the ones who built the most trust before the conversation started.

Which means the highest-leverage marketing investment right now isn't the bottom-of-funnel ad or the outbound sequence. It's whatever builds reputation among the buyers who haven't raised their hand yet.

They're making their list. Are you on it?
