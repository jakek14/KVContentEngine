## Post

Meta changed click-through attribution this month.

Real link clicks only. Likes, saves, shares, and comments are now tracked separately under something called "Engage-through attribution."

A lot of advertisers are going to look at their dashboards this week and think their performance dropped.

It didn't.

The metric finally caught up with reality. What used to count as a "click" on paper was never actually sending anyone to your website. You were optimizing for a number that was partially made up.

Honest data is uncomfortable when you've been reporting inflated numbers to stakeholders. But it's the starting point for actually improving.

If your CTR "dropped" this month -- that's worth understanding before you touch the budget.

What's your take -- is this change going to force hard conversations with clients or leadership?
