## Post

Only 54% of marketing emails reach Gmail's primary inbox.

The rest land in promotions, spam, or disappear entirely.

And the open rate data most teams are using to judge performance? Apple Mail Privacy Protection has inflated it by 15 to 20 percentage points since late 2021. If your open rates jumped in Q4 2021 and never came back down, that's not your content getting better. That's Apple auto-loading tracking pixels.

Here's what to measure instead.

Click-to-open rate (CTOR) is the metric that held up. It measures clicks as a percentage of opens -- filtering out the ghost opens Apple generates. If your CTOR is trending up, your content is resonating with people who actually see it. If CTOR is flat while open rates stay high, you have a problem you haven't diagnosed yet.

Reply rate matters more than open rate for outbound sequences. A reply -- even "not interested" -- confirms deliverability to that address and sends a positive signal to inbox providers. Sequences optimized for replies outperform sequences optimized for clicks in most B2B contexts.

Deliverability hygiene is the real unlock. Full SPF/DKIM/DMARC authentication is the minimum in 2026 -- not a competitive advantage, just table stakes. Beyond that: domain reputation monitoring, removing unengaged addresses every 90 days, and dedicated sending infrastructure for high-volume sends.

The teams doing well in email right now mostly didn't change how they write. They changed what they measure, cleaned their lists, and got serious about deliverability.

54% primary inbox placement is a floor, not a ceiling. The gap between average senders and best-in-class is almost entirely an infrastructure and hygiene story -- not a content story.
