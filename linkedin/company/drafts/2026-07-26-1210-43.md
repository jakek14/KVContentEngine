## Post

Your retargeting audience is smaller than you think.

Not because your traffic dropped. Because the cookies did.

Chrome completed its third-party cookie restrictions. Safari and Firefox already had. The result: a significant chunk of your website visitors never get added to your retargeting pools in Google, Meta, or LinkedIn -- because the tracking pixel couldn't fire or the browser blocked it.

Most companies look at their retargeting campaign performance and assume the audience list is accurate. It isn't.

Here's what that actually means: your remarketing list represents a subset of the people who visited. The ones it misses are invisible. They browsed your product, hit your pricing page, compared you to competitors, and left. No cookie. No retargeting. No follow-up.

Three things worth doing now.

Audit your retargeting pool size against your actual traffic volume. If the ratio is significantly off, you have a tracking gap that's distorting your remarketing spend.

Shift toward consent-based identification methods that don't rely on third-party cookies -- email capture, gated content, first-party data from your own platform. These don't expire when a browser policy changes.

Diversify into email-based audience matching. Platforms like LinkedIn Matched Audiences and Meta Custom Audiences built from email lists are more durable than cookie-based pools. The match persists across devices and browsers because the key doesn't expire.

Companies that treat their retargeting list as reliable are optimizing to a fraction of their real market and making decisions on incomplete data. The ones who audit the gap make better decisions.
