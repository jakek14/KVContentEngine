## Post

General Motors paid $12.75 million this month for selling driver location data without consent.

Not a hack. Not a breach.

A business decision -- GM's OnStar service had been sharing location and driving behavior data with insurers. Customers weren't clearly told. California's AG pursued it under CCPA.

The fine is a signal.

Most companies aren't running complex data monetization operations. But a lot of them have data practices that were built in 2019 -- before CCPA enforcement sharpened, before 20 US states added their own privacy laws, before regulators staffed up to actually pursue cases.

Things that were common in 2020 are enforcement targets now.

Data collected without clear disclosure. Third-party sharing without opt-in. Marketing data retention policies that were never formally set. Lead data sitting in a CRM with no documented consent trail.

This isn't a compliance scare story. It's a flag for teams that haven't audited their data practices since the last fundraise.

The GM settlement was news. A fine against a smaller company won't get the same coverage -- but it'll be just as real.

If your data practices haven't been reviewed recently, that review is overdue.
