## Post

Marketing and sales tools have the highest churn of any SaaS category.

Monthly logo churn of 4.8% to 8.1%. That's from 2026 benchmarks across 939 B2B SaaS companies. Infrastructure tools -- databases, dev ops, cloud storage -- come in below 1.8%.

That's a 3 to 4x difference in retention by category.

The gap isn't about product quality. It's about what each type of tool promises.

Infrastructure tools embed in your workflow. They handle a function. When they go away, something breaks. So they rarely go away -- and when they're good, customers don't think about replacing them.

Marketing tools promise outcomes. More leads. Better conversions. Higher ROAS. The problem: they're selling outputs that share credit with a hundred other variables -- your creative, your offer, your market timing, your sales team, the season.

When results disappoint, the marketing tool is the easiest line item to cut. It's never fully the tool's fault. But it's never clearly not the tool's fault either.

The marketing software companies that beat this churn pattern have one thing in common: they're embedded in data workflows, not in campaign outcomes.

CRM, email infrastructure, attribution, first-party data collection -- these categories show dramatically lower churn than campaign management tools. The value is in the data layer. Not the campaign layer.

For B2B buyers: the closer a tool is to raw data and workflow, the more it compounds over time. The closer it is to campaign outputs, the more it has to keep proving itself at every renewal.

For founders building marketing software: the churn clock is part of the product problem. If your tool can't embed in a data workflow, you're competing on quarterly outcomes instead of compounding value. That's a harder business to build and a harder sale to keep.

The retention benchmarks tell you what the market already figured out.

Worth reading them backward.
