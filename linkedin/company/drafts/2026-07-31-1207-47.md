## Post

Community members retain at 40% higher rates than non-community customers.

That's not a brand-building metric. That's a revenue metric.

Here's what we're seeing from the B2B companies running this well in 2026:

A peer network where customers help each other solve problems. Not a support channel -- a Slack group or Discord where power users share workflows, compare results, and ask each other questions before they'd ever open a ticket. The product becomes stickier because the people around the product become valuable too.

Content that comes from community members, not just the marketing team. User-generated case studies, implementation threads, "here's how we use this for X" breakdowns. Buyers trust other buyers at a rate brand content can't match -- 4x higher conversion in some categories.

An expansion signal hiding in plain sight. Your most engaged community members are also your best candidates for upsell and referral. The signal is already there in their behavior. Most teams just aren't reading it.

The B2B brands that built this three years ago are compounding now. Not because community is magic -- but because the retention advantage stacks over time.

Starting doesn't require a full platform build. It requires one question: where do your best customers already gather, and can you facilitate something useful there?

What does community look like in your space right now -- are you running one, or is this still on the roadmap?
