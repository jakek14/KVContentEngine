## Post

The thing about doing marketing at a bootstrapped company: you can't outspend the competition.

That sounds like a disadvantage. For a while, it is.

But something shifts. When you can't buy your way to results, you get really good at finding what actually moves the needle. Every channel has to justify itself. You stop running experiments just to have data -- you run them to answer specific questions.

You learn to think before you test, not after.

I went from doing everything manually -- spreadsheets, Canva, tasks that ate entire afternoons -- to building systems that handle the repetitive stuff so I can focus on the parts that require actual judgment. AI didn't replace the work. It freed me to do the harder version of it.

That's the shift I didn't expect. It's not "AI does marketing." It's "AI handles the parts that didn't need a human anyway, so you can spend more time on the parts that do."

Small team. No budget to hide behind. Still moving faster than a lot of marketing operations I've come across.

That's the only edge we have. We use it.
