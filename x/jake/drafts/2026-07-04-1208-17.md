## Post

Apple Mail preloads tracking pixels before anyone actually opens the email.

49% of all email opens happen in Apple Mail. When the pixel fires on preload, it gets recorded as an open -- whether the person read the email or not.

That's why industry-wide open rates sit at 42-44%. The real number is 20-21%.

If you're A/B testing subject lines against open rate, you're optimizing against a metric that's roughly half phantom data.

What actually tells you something: click rate (2.27% average), revenue per send, list-level unsubscribes. Those don't have an Apple problem.

Open rate still matters for one thing: if it drops suddenly, you have a deliverability issue. But as a creative performance metric? Mostly noise.

Measure the thing that moves revenue.

## Context

Apple Mail Privacy Protection, introduced in iOS 15 and still active in 2026, preloads email tracking pixels as part of its privacy features. With Apple Mail accounting for ~49% of all email opens, this systematically inflates reported open rates. The real industry average is ~20.73% vs the commonly cited 42-44%. Jake's angle: this is a fundamental measurement problem most marketers don't know about, or know about but haven't internalized into how they evaluate campaigns. Primary marketing-insights pillar -- this is pure marketing craft, no AI framing, no KV angle. No fabricated personal details -- all industry-documented data points.
