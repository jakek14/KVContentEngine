## Post

Everyone can build now.

Cursor has 1 million monthly active users. 34% of new micro-SaaS products that launched in Q1 were shipped by founders who had never written code before.

The full tool cost is about $55 a month.

That should feel like good news. And it is. But it also means the thing that used to separate successful products from dead ones isn't build quality anymore.

It's distribution. It's knowing exactly who you're building for. It's having somewhere real to put the thing when it's done.

As someone focused on the marketing side of a small bootstrapped company, this shift is impossible to miss. The conversations that make the difference aren't "what should we build next." They're "who exactly is this for and how do we reach them."

The build got 10x faster. The distribution problem stayed exactly the same.

What's your take -- does faster building actually change what you decide to build?
