## Post

Signal-first isn't just a cold email tactic.

It's the right way to think about most marketing.

The worst-performing campaigns I've run were the ones where we had a solid list, decent copy, strong creative -- and no evidence that anyone in that audience was actively thinking about the problem we solve.

You can get all the mechanical stuff right and still just be talking to people at the wrong moment.

The campaigns that overperform aren't always the most polished ones. They're the ones where someone gave us a real signal before we reached out -- they engaged with content, searched a specific term, made a hire that matched a pattern we recognize.

We followed up while it was still relevant. That's the difference.

Troy's framing applies to cold email. But it also applies to paid retargeting, to content strategy, to when you follow up after a sales call. Timing isn't a tactical detail. It's the whole thing.

Most marketing teams optimize for the message. The ones winning are optimizing for the moment.
