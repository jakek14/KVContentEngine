## Post

**Tweet 1**
Google just replaced 20 years of campaign management with a text field.

Here's what it actually means for marketers.

**Tweet 2**
AI Max = write a brief in plain language, describe your creative vision and targeting guardrails. The AI handles keywords, bids, assets, audience. Everything that took years to master is now a prompt.

**Tweet 3**
Reaction I keep seeing: "campaign managers are getting replaced."

Probably yes, at the execution layer. But automated bidding already killed manual bid management years ago. This is the continuation, not the start.

**Tweet 4**
What AI Max can't do: tell you if your brief is right.

Describe the wrong customer and it optimizes toward the wrong outcome — faster than you could before.

**Tweet 5**
The job shifted from "know how to configure campaigns" to "know your customer well enough to brief an AI."

That's always been the actual marketing job. The interface just finally caught up.

**Tweet 6**
If you can't describe your customer's real problem in a short paragraph, you weren't going to win on the old setup either.

The brief just made the gap visible.

## Context

Google launched AI Max as a campaign type that replaces the 20-year-old paradigm of keyword lists, manual bids, and granular audience configuration with a natural-language brief. Marketers who built careers around technical campaign management are watching those skills commoditize. Jake's angle as a marketer: agree this is real disruption at the execution layer, extend to the deeper truth — the brief is just the customer understanding problem made explicit. The judgment and customer knowledge that were always the hard part of marketing are now the only differentiator.
