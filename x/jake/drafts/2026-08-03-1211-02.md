## Post

Every dollar in paid ads disappears the moment you stop spending.

That's not a knock on paid. It's just the mechanic.

Email works differently. The list is there tomorrow whether you ran ads today or not. People subscribed because they wanted to hear from you. No algorithm decides who sees it.

Paid is the engine for now. Email is the engine for later. Most marketers treat email as the follow-up to paid. The ones doing it right treat email as the asset and paid as the thing that feeds it.

When traffic hits your site, where does it go? Into a list you own -- or a retargeting pool you rent?

## Context

D2C ecommerce brands have 5 years of rising CAC as a case study. The ones holding acquisition costs down are building owned channels (email, community, referral) and using paid as the amplifier rather than the primary engine. Jake's post takes this principle and frames it as a marketer's observation -- the key distinction between channels you build vs. channels you rent. Clean, practical, no fabricated details.
