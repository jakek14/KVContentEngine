## Post

Everyone's talking about this for coding. The exact same thing is happening in marketing.

A study found experienced developers were 19% slower with AI tools -- the overhead of supervising the AI ate up the speed gains.

Marketing runs the same way.

If you understand positioning, audience, and what makes a message land -- AI lets you move through those decisions faster. Iterate creative in a morning instead of waiting a week for data to roll in.

If you don't have that foundation, AI doesn't give it to you. It just lets you be wrong at scale.

The marketers getting the most out of these tools aren't the ones outsourcing the thinking. They're the ones doing more of the thinking they were already doing well.

The skill ceiling didn't move. The floor got raised. The gap between good and average just got wider.
