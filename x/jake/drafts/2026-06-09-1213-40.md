## Post

Email is getting harder to send.

Not because the rules changed. Because the scoring did.

Gmail moved from 'delay and warn' to permanently rejecting non-compliant bulk senders last November. Authentication -- DKIM, SPF, DMARC -- used to be optional. Now it's the price of admission. Skip it and your emails don't land in spam. They don't land at all.

But authentication isn't the hard part anymore.

Fully authenticated mail still hits spam more than 30% of the time. Because once Gmail confirms who you are, it asks the harder question: does anyone actually want this email?

The new flow: Gmail shows your campaign to a small sample of your list first. If that sample reads it, the rest gets it. If they scroll past, the algorithm decides your content isn't worth surfacing -- and quietly buries future sends.

Same logic social platforms have run for years. Relevance wins. List size stops mattering if list quality is low.

Which completely changes the math on segmentation. A smaller list of people who genuinely want your emails outperforms a huge list of people who technically opted in two years ago and haven't opened since.

The marketers who haven't cleaned their lists recently are running on borrowed time. And treating 'email strategy' as separate from 'content strategy' is solving the wrong problem.
