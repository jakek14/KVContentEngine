## Post

The average CTR on paid ads is 2.27%.

Top 10% of ads hit 5.22%.

Same audience. Same targeting. Same budget. More than double the performance.

The difference is almost never the audience. It's the creative.

I've been paying attention to what separates ads that work from ads that just look good. A few patterns keep showing up.

The first frame does everything. You have maybe 1.5 seconds before someone scrolls. Most ads use those seconds to show a logo or a product shot. The ones that actually convert use them to interrupt a thought -- something unexpected, something that raises a question.

One promise, not three. The instinct is to pack in as many selling points as possible. The best-performing ads pick one thing and own it completely. 'This cuts your prospecting time in half.' Not 'boost engagement, save time, grow revenue, and more.'

Native beats polished. An ad that looks like an ad gets treated like an ad -- skipped. An ad that looks like content gets watched. This isn't a trick. It's how attention actually works.

None of this is new. But I'm still surprised by how many brands skip it and go straight to 'we need a bigger budget.'

The budget conversation is easy. Everyone wants to spend more to reach more people.

The creative conversation is harder. It means admitting most of your ads aren't good enough to deserve more spend.

What's the best ad creative you've seen recently? Always looking for examples to learn from.
