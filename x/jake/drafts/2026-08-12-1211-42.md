## Post

Big marketing budgets hide bad strategy.

When you have unlimited spend to test, you figure out what works eventually. When budget is tight, you figure it out faster -- because you can't afford not to.

Limited budget means you ask "is this the right audience?" before you launch. "Is this message actually clear?" before you put dollars behind it. "Is this channel where our buyer actually lives?" before you commit.

That's not a disadvantage. That's just what good marketing looks like.

Working at a bootstrapped company forced me to get good at prioritization before I got good at execution. I'm not sure I would have learned that somewhere with unlimited resources and a quarterly testing budget to burn through.

The constraint is the teacher.
