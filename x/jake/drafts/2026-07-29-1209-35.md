## Post

This hits different from a marketing perspective.

Most companies treat retention as a CS problem. Marketing acquires, CS keeps. Clean handoff, clean responsibility.

But if the old playbook isn't working -- great product, fast time-to-value, strong adoption -- the question becomes: what actually keeps customers now?

Part of the answer is marketing.

The customers who stay long-term usually came in with the right expectations. They understood what the product does. They weren't sold something that doesn't exist.

That's a marketing problem before it ever becomes a CS problem.

Retention starts at the top of the funnel. It always did. Most teams just didn't notice because the acquisition engine was refilling the bucket fast enough to hide the hole.

If churn is your real problem, I'd look at messaging before I'd look at onboarding flows.
