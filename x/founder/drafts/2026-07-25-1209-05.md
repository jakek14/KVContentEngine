## Post

"SaaS isn't coming back" is the Crunchbase headline this week.

Half right.

What's dying is the category of software people log into daily to do tasks an AI agent could handle for $20/month. That software made sense when automation was expensive. Now the cost of an agent that just does the work is lower than the SaaS subscription for the tool that helps you do it.

What's not dying: software embedded in critical workflows, holding proprietary data, or making judgment calls the AI still can't reliably make.

The companies getting funded in 2026 aren't SaaS companies in the old sense. They're AI companies with a subscription pricing model. Sounds semantic. It's not. One builds a tool you use. The other does the work for you.

If you're building right now, the question isn't "how do I make my product stickier." It's "at what point does the AI just do this directly -- and what breaks in my product when that happens."

Most founders haven't asked it yet.
