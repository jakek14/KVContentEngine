## Post

61% of SaaS companies now have a usage-based component.

Up from 27% in 2021.

That's not a pricing experiment anymore. That's the market telling you what it will tolerate.

Seat-based pricing made sense when software was a one-time procurement decision. IT signed the contract. Lock-in was built into the deal structure. Buyers had limited options and high switching costs.

That world is mostly gone.

Buyers have tried three tools in your category this year. They've been burned by shelfware. They know what a bad deal looks like. And they're not signing multi-seat contracts for software their team might not actually adopt.

Usage-based changes the calculus. The buyer pays for value they get. The vendor has every incentive to drive adoption -- because low usage means low revenue. Customer risk drops. Deals close faster.

The companies on usage-based are growing 38% faster. Not because the software is better. Because the incentives are aligned.

If you're still selling seats, one question: who benefits most from that structure? If the honest answer is "us, not the customer" -- that's worth sitting with.
