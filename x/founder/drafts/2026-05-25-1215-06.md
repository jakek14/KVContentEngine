## Post

10 state attorneys general just pooled their enforcement resources into a single privacy consortium.

Shared intelligence. Coordinated investigations. First joint settlement hit three states simultaneously.

For two years I watched founders treat data privacy like a compliance tax — something to handle later, something to outsource, something that would never really matter until it did.

Here's the reframe:

The companies scrambling right now to tear out third-party pixels, re-architect their retargeting stack, and rewrite vendor contracts aren't in trouble because they got unlucky.

They're in trouble because they built on infrastructure they didn't own.

The teams that spent 2023 and 2024 building clean first-party data practices — capturing identity directly, building owned audiences, structuring their data collection for compliance — didn't just reduce legal exposure.

They built something most competitors now have to spend months and real capital to replicate.

Compliance overhead quietly became a structural moat.

Most founders never make that reframe until the enforcement pressure forces it.

The window to make it voluntarily is closing fast.
