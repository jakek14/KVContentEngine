## Post

The old SaaS retention playbook: great product, fast time to value, high adoption, strong uptime.

Nick Mehta nails it -- that's not enough anymore.

Here's what I'd add: that playbook worked because switching had real friction. Data migration. Retraining. Integration rebuilds. Weeks of pain.

AI is erasing that friction. A competitor can pitch your customer today and an agent can move their entire workflow by Friday.

The new retention isn't about how good your product is. It's about how embedded you are in a process they'd have to redesign to replace you.

Product quality gets you in the door. Workflow depth keeps you there.

https://x.com/nrmehta/status/2036495922503594308

## Context

Nick Mehta (CEO of Gainsight) posted about a $140M ARR SaaS company where the old customer success playbook is no longer working. This is a great agree-and-extend opportunity: the reason the old playbook is failing is structural -- AI is compressing switching costs in ways that make product quality a baseline expectation, not a moat. Timely and directly relevant to any SaaS founder thinking about retention.
