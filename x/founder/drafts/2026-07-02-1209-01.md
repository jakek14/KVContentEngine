## Post

70% of SaaS churn happens in the first 90 days.

Not month 12. Not after a price increase. Not when a competitor shows up with a better offer.

The first 90 days.

Most founders are solving a retention problem that is actually an onboarding problem they haven't diagnosed yet.

The metric that predicts it better than anything: time-to-first-value. The gap between when a customer signs up and when they get the first outcome they actually paid for.

Customers who hit first value in under 7 days retain at 80%+ at month 12.

Customers who don't hit it within their first 30 days retain at 35 to 50%.

That's not a marginal difference. That's a cliff.

The companies spending on re-engagement campaigns, win-back sequences, and churn-reduction tooling -- while skipping the onboarding -- are treating the symptom. The cause is upstream.

Here's the exercise worth running: what is the single moment where a new customer first "gets it"? The first sync that works. The first report that shows them something they didn't already know. The first conversion logged.

Now count how many new signups reach that moment within 7 days. And what's in the way for the ones who don't.

That's the problem worth solving. Everything else is downstream of it.
