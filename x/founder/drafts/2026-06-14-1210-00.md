## Post

**Tweet 1**
AI-native SaaS tools under $50/month are retaining 23% of gross revenue.

Not 23% of users. 23% of revenue.

That's the "AI tourist" problem. And it's about to hit a lot of founders hard.

**Tweet 2**
An AI tourist signs up for one task. Uses the tool exactly for that. Hits their goal. Leaves.

The product worked perfectly. And it still churned.

That's not a support issue. It's a category problem.

**Tweet 3**
The pricing data makes the point clearly.

Under $50/month: 23% gross revenue retention.

Above $250/month: 70-85%.

That gap isn't features. It's commitment. High price forces integration. Integration creates switching costs.

**Tweet 4**
If someone can replace your product with a ChatGPT plugin in 20 minutes, you don't have a product.

You have a workflow shortcut.

Shortcuts get replaced.

The question: what would it actually cost your customer to stop using you?

**Tweet 5**
Not in dollars. In workflow disruption. In decisions that would happen manually. In signals they'd lose.

That's the durability test for any SaaS in 2026 -- including why I built KnownVisitors. The more founders know who's visiting and converting, the harder that signal is to give up.

Build for that cost.
