## Post

$1.35 million.

That's the settlement Tractor Supply just paid the California Privacy Protection Agency for deploying tracking pixels before getting explicit consent.

PlayOn Sports caught the same fine. Different company, same mistake -- tracking users and serving targeted ads without a real opt-out mechanism.

The CPPA is not playing around in 2026. $7,500 per intentional violation. $2,500 per unintentional. And they're now auditing proactively, not just responding to complaints.

Most founders treat this like a legal problem. Wrong framing. It's a data strategy problem.

But when you rely on third-party pixels and cookies, you're building on sand. iOS 17 broke link tracking. GDPR gutted clean data in Europe. CCPA is now following through in the US.

The companies getting this right aren't the ones with the most tracking. They're the ones with the most trustworthy data.

Consent-based. Verified. Owned.

The $1.35M fine is a headline. The quieter story is the thousands of companies that never get fined -- but also never figure out who's actually on their site.

They just never know.
