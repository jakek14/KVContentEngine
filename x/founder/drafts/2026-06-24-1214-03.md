## Post

The part people focus on is the buses. That's the outcome.

The underrated part is what "bootstrap it and refuse VC" actually required over 10 years.

No external money means no cushion to spend your way to product-market fit. You find it by listening to customers -- because they're the only people who can keep you alive. Every dollar spent is one already earned. Every decision gets made with a shorter runway and higher stakes.

The constraint is what makes the product better.

Companies with funding can afford to ignore bad signals. More experiments, more hires, more time. Bootstrapped companies can't. So they develop a different discipline -- one most VC-backed founders only learn after the money runs out.

A decade of that discipline gets you on 50 buses.
