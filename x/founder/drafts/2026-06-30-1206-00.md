## Post

Meta is projected to out-earn Google in digital ad revenue this year.

$243 billion to $239 billion. First time in the history of online advertising.

For a decade, "where do we put paid budget" defaulted to Google first, Meta second. The logic made sense -- people search for what they want on Google, catch them there. Meta was for awareness. Harder to measure, lower intent.

That assumption is getting expensive to hold onto.

Meta's Advantage+ suite has closed the attribution gap significantly. AI-optimized creative is running campaigns most marketers couldn't build manually. And with zero-click search eating into Google's organic AND paid traffic quality, Google's moat is thinner than it looked two years ago.

None of this means switch everything to Meta.

It means the default assumption -- that Google owns performance and Meta owns awareness -- is worth challenging with your own data.

The best founders I know don't have a "this is where we advertise" answer. They have a "here's what our unit economics say this week" answer.

The platforms are converging. The edge goes to whoever tests faster.

## Context

eMarketer projects Meta to surpass Google in global digital ad revenue for the first time in 2026. Meta's Advantage+ AI-powered advertising suite has been the single biggest driver of this shift. The real takeaway: test assumptions constantly rather than defaulting to channel hierarchy.
