## Post

J.B. is right.

Per-seat pricing wasn't just a commercial decision. It was a worldview. Software exists to serve humans who log in, click around, and produce value. Charge per person.

Agents don't take seats. They make API calls at weird hours, complete tasks without logging in, and have no concept of a dashboard. The per-seat assumption -- that the "user" is a human -- is breaking.

Most SaaS founders haven't worked through what this means for their pricing. They should. Before their customers force the conversation.
