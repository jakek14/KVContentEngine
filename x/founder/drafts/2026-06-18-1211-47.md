## Post

70% of SaaS churn happens in the first 90 days.

Not from a bad product update. Not from a competitor winning the account.

The decision was made in week one.

Most founders respond to churn by building better docs, adding support seats, improving the product. All of that matters. But it's solving the problem at the wrong end of the funnel.

The real question is: did your customer ever actually experience the thing your product is built to do?

The average activation rate for B2B SaaS sits at 37.5%. Roughly two out of three new signups never reach the core value the product was designed to deliver. They signed up, poked around, got confused or got busy, and were already halfway gone before the first invoice.

That's not a retention problem. It's an activation problem with a retention outcome.

Three things that actually move the number:

Shorten the path to the first win. Customers who hit first value inside 14 days retain at 80%+ at month 12. The ones who don't get there in 30 days retain at 35-50%. That gap lives entirely in onboarding. Map the shortest path from signup to one specific, meaningful outcome -- not 12 features, just one. Cut everything else out of the way.

Make the welcome sequence a job aid, not a tour. Most onboarding emails are user manuals. Nobody rereads a manual. The sequence should answer one question: what do I do in the next 20 minutes to get value? That's it. One answer. Everything else is noise.

Pull the behavioral data from 30-day churners. Not support tickets -- actual product usage logs. Where did they stop? When did they stop logging in? The answer is almost always visible in the data before anyone filed a cancellation. Find the drop-off. That's where the problem lives.

Acquisition gets the budget, the headlines, and the team meetings.

Onboarding is where the business is actually won or lost.
