## Post

The middle of the VC market disappeared.

Not slowly. In 2026.

For years, the path looked like this:
→ Raise seed
→ Build for 18–24 months
→ Hit traction
→ Series A

That path still exists. But almost exclusively for AI-native companies.

For everyone else?

Seed rounds are harder to close. The Series A bar — $1-2M ARR, 110%+ NRR, CAC payback under 12 months — hasn't changed. But the comparison class has.

VCs are watching AI-native companies hit those numbers in half the time. The contrast is brutal.

What's left for founders in 2026:

If you're building AI infrastructure — raise aggressively. The window is real.

If you're building vertical SaaS with clear workflow value — you can still raise, but revenue has to come before capital now.

If you're building something useful that doesn't fit the AI thesis — bootstrap to revenue.

Most founders treat that third path like a consolation prize.

It's not.

Bootstrap forces you to find customers who actually pay on day one. It removes the "we'll figure out monetization later" trap that VC money creates. The constraint becomes the discipline.

90% of bootstrapped companies that reach $1M ARR are still operating 10 years later. The VC-backed survival rate doesn't come close.

VC bifurcation isn't a crisis.

It's a sorting mechanism.

The question in 2026 isn't whether you can raise.

It's whether you should.

## Context

SaaS funding landscape has bifurcated dramatically in 2026. AI mega-rounds dominate; traditional SaaS seed funding is contracting. Most bootstrapped founder content focuses on scrappiness — this angles on it as a rational strategic choice given current funding reality.
