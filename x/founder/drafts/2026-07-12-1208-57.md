## Post

If your Meta ROAS dropped in the last few months without an obvious cause, this is probably why.

Meta changed how it counts conversions. Likes, saves, and shares no longer qualify as click-through. Only actual link clicks count now.

A lot of accounts saw reported ROAS fall 15-30% overnight. Not because performance changed. Because the definition of a conversion got honest.

The right move isn't to cut budget. It's to audit what those numbers actually meant before -- and decide if the campaigns that "worked" back then were actually working.

## Context

Meta's attribution update (March 2026 rollout) changed click-through to require actual link clicks. Non-link interactions moved to "engage-through attribution" with a 1-day window. Many advertisers are seeing reported ROAS drop 15-30% in Ads Manager without a real performance change. The founder's take: this is good news -- inflated metrics led to bad budget decisions.
