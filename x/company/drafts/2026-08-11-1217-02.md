## Post

California's DROP platform went live August 1.

500+ registered data brokers are now legally required to check consumer opt-out requests every 45 days and delete accordingly.

One click. Every broker. Gone.

The cold email list you bought last month? Some of those contacts already opted out. You don't know which ones. You won't know until deliverability tanks and you're running campaigns to people who legally asked not to be reached.

This is where the first-party vs third-party data divide starts mattering at the P&L level.

The companies building contact lists from their own traffic -- visitors who showed up on their site with genuine intent -- they're immune to DROP. That data isn't sitting in a broker's database waiting to be deleted. It's theirs.

We built KnownVisitors to give companies exactly that. Name, email, phone, company -- from real visitors who actually came to your site and showed up voluntarily.

The list that can't be legislated away from you is the one you own.

That's the data worth building on. Especially right now.

## Context

California's DELETE Act and DROP platform (Delete Request and Opt-Out Platform) went live August 1, 2026, requiring data brokers to process consumer deletion requests every 45 days. This is a significant inflection point for outbound marketing strategies relying on bought lists.
