## Post

The typical B2B SaaS company spends $2 in sales and marketing to acquire every $1 of new ARR.

Top quartile recovers that cost in 6 months. Bottom quartile takes 24.

Same product type. Wildly different outcomes. The difference usually isn't the acquisition channel.

It's what happens after the contract signs.

Top-quartile companies grow accounts after the sale -- NRR above 110% compresses payback faster than any top-of-funnel change. They also get customers to value quickly. A customer who doesn't see results in the first 30 days rarely makes it to renewal.

Bottom-quartile companies are often selling to too broad an ICP. Mismatched customers churn. Churn forces you to replace them -- which keeps the cost high no matter how efficient the acquisition.

If your payback math isn't working, more spend rarely fixes it. Sharper targeting and faster time-to-value usually do.
