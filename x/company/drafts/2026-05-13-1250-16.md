## Post

Meta just changed how it counts conversions.

Click-through attribution now only includes link clicks. Likes, shares, saves, comments — those no longer count.

For most accounts, reported conversions dropped 15–40% overnight.

Your ads didn't get worse. The measurement shifted.

Here's the harder truth: this will happen again.

Platforms adjust how they report. They always have. The brands that panic when numbers move are the ones who built their entire measurement stack on what a platform tells them.

First-party signals don't move when Meta updates its methodology.

CRM data. Revenue attribution. Actual pipeline. Behavioral signals on your own site.

That layer holds.

When the platform changes the rules, your own data doesn't change with it.

## Context

Meta's attribution change (March/April 2026) redefined click-through to include only link clicks, causing reported conversions to drop 15-40% across millions of ad accounts overnight — not due to performance decline but a measurement shift. The saas-advice angle: this is a structural reminder that measurement built on platform-reported metrics is inherently fragile. First-party data is the only layer that's immune to platform redefinitions.
