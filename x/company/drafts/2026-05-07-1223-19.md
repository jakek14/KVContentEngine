## Post

Meta just changed what counts as a conversion click.

Likes, saves, and comments no longer count toward attributed conversions. Only actual link clicks do.

Advertisers running Meta campaigns are watching their reported ROAS numbers drop this month.

Before anyone panics: the campaigns didn't get worse.

The old attribution model was giving advertisers credit for engagements that never drove purchase intent. A like on an ad doesn't mean someone bought anything. A save doesn't mean a sale. Meta's attribution was just pretending otherwise.

Better measurement leads to better decisions. The advertisers who understand this will reallocate budget to what's actually working. The ones who don't will spend the next quarter trying to "recover" to ROAS numbers that were never real.

Honest data is always a competitive advantage.

The confusion is temporary. The clarity is permanent.

## Context

Meta announced a tightening of click-through attribution in 2026 — only actual link clicks count toward conversion attribution, while interactions (likes, comments, shares, saves) are reclassified as "engage-through" instead. This will cause apparent ROAS drops for many advertisers. Hot-take: this is a measurement correction, not a performance decline. The advertisers who recognize this first will gain an advantage in optimization decisions.
