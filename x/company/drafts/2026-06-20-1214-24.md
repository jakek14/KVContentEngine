## Post

Only 30% of CMOs are confident they can actually measure marketing ROI.

That means 70% are making budget decisions on incomplete data.

Attribution has been quietly breaking for years. iOS 14 hit click tracking. Third-party cookies are mostly gone. Multi-device journeys don't resolve cleanly. Last-click models credit the final Google ad even when five other touchpoints did the real work.

Three things that still tell the truth when the attribution data doesn't:

Revenue-tied metrics, not clicks. MQL-to-close rate by source. Email revenue per send. Cost per dollar collected, not cost per lead. These tie directly to money you can count -- not attributed impressions you can't verify.

Incrementality tests. Turn off a channel for a cohort for two weeks. Does revenue move? If not, the channel wasn't doing what the dashboard claimed. Uncomfortable but accurate.

Ask your last 20 customers how they actually found you. Not through a form. Real conversations. The answers will conflict with your attribution model. They'll almost always be more accurate.

The brands making smart budget decisions aren't the ones with better attribution stacks. They're the ones treating their data critically.
