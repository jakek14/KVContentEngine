## Post

Meta changed how it counts conversions in March.

Before: any click — like, share, save, image tap — counted toward click-through attribution.

Now: only actual link clicks count. Everything else goes to a separate category with a 1-day window.

What this looks like in your dashboard: remarketing campaigns down 20-35%. Brand awareness down 40-50%.

What actually happened to your business: nothing.

The spend didn't change. The customers didn't change. The way results are counted did.

If your team panicked at March numbers and cut campaigns — check your engage-through attribution column. It's probably full of conversions that used to live somewhere else.

Don't move budget based on a reporting change.

Make sure you understand what you're actually seeing before you touch anything.

## Context

Meta's March 2026 attribution update narrowed click-through attribution to include only actual link clicks to websites, apps, lead forms, or shops. Likes, comments, saves, and profile taps were reclassified as "engage-through attribution" with a much shorter 1-day window. This caused many advertisers to see dramatic reported conversion drops without any actual decline in business performance.
